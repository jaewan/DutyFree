# W7.2 — A1's LLC is 20 MiB, not 32 MiB, and its bandwidth reference is LLC-resident

Written 2026-08-24, **while the 24 morsel cells are still running and before any
of them has produced a stats file.** Only the four `stream-smoke` reference runs
have completed. Nothing here can have been shaped by a W7 result, which is the
same standard `W7_PREREGISTRATION_2026-08-23.md` §6.3 sets for Knob A itself.

Zero compute cost: four completed reference runs, `config.ini`, and gem5 source.

## Finding 1 — the configured 32 MiB LLC is realized as 20 MiB

`w7_campaign.sh` passes `--l3_size=32MiB --l3_assoc=20` for A1, and
`config.ini` confirms `system.ruby.hnf.cntrl.cache size=33554432 assoc=20`. That
is not the capacity the run has. `CacheMemory::init()` in
`src/mem/ruby/structures/CacheMemory.cc:105-109`:

```
m_cache_num_sets = (m_cache_size / m_cache_assoc) / m_block_size;
m_cache_num_set_bits = floorLog2(m_cache_num_sets);
```

and `CacheMemory::addressToCacheSet` indexes with `m_cache_num_set_bits`, so only
`2^floorLog2(num_sets)` sets are ever addressed. The remainder are allocated and
unreachable.

| point | requested | `num_sets` | `floorLog2` | sets indexed | **effective LLC** |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A0 | 5 MiB / 20-way | 4096 | 12 | 4096 | **5 MiB — exact** |
| A1 | 32 MiB / 20-way | 26,214 | 14 | 16,384 | **20 MiB (62.5%)** |

9,830 sets — 37.5% of A1's tag and data arrays — are allocated and never
indexed. Every other cache in the campaign is exact: L1d 49152/12 = 64 sets,
L1i 32768/8 = 64, L2 2 MiB/16 = 2048 and 512 KiB/16 = 512, all powers of two.
A1's LLC is the only affected structure.

This is **F9's defect class** — a declared size silently quantized to a power of
two — relocated from the hot table to the LLC.

**Two pre-registration statements are wrong as realized.** §2 Knob A says
"L2 512 KiB/core, LLC 32 MiB → 64×" and "victim 8 MiB (25% of LLC)". The run
gives **40×** and **40%**.

**32 MiB is not reachable at 20-way.** At assoc 20 the attainable sizes are
`2^k × 20 × 64`: 20 MiB (`k=14`) then 40 MiB (`k=15`). Reaching 32 MiB requires
assoc 16 (`32 MiB/16/64 = 32768 = 2^15`, exact), and `w7_campaign.sh`'s header
states associativity is deliberately held. So the options are 20 MiB @ 20-way
(40×), 40 MiB @ 20-way (80×), or 32 MiB @ 16-way (64×, the pre-registered ratio,
at the cost of the pinned associativity). None is free and the choice is a
pre-registration amendment, not an analysis decision.

## Finding 2 — A1's stream-smoke reference is not a streaming reference

The four completed reference runs, `--mode stream-smoke`, fact array 16 MiB,
1 warmup + 3 reps:

| cell | bench GB/s | CXL read | passes over the fact array | HNF demand hit | HNF fills |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A0 SMOKE wb | 3.960 | 84.40 MB | 5.03 | 0.27% | 1,314,720 |
| A0 SMOKE stream | 4.609 | 65.21 MB | 3.89 | 27.03% | 298,569 |
| A1 SMOKE wb | 9.273 | **16.78 MB** | **1.00** | **97.98%** | 321,818 |
| A1 SMOKE stream | 9.270 | **16.78 MB** | **1.00** | **97.99%** | 321,815 |

At A1 the fact array is read from CXL **exactly once** — 16,777,216 bytes, to
the byte, in both arms — and the remaining three passes are served on chip. The
16 MiB array fits the 20 MiB effective LLC. **9.27 GB/s is an on-chip
bandwidth, not an achievable CXL streaming bandwidth**, and it cannot serve as
the denominator §3 P2 calls "achieved fused bandwidth."

A0's reference is sound by the same test: 5.03 passes of CXL traffic under WB
against a 4-pass compulsory floor, i.e. the array does not fit and WB
additionally re-reads ~25% of it.

**P2 is unaffected.** It is evaluated at A0/B1 and uses the A0 reference.

## Finding 3 — what the A1 arms show about H2, stated carefully

At A1 the `wb` and `stream` arms are indistinguishable: CXL bytes identical to
the byte, HNF fills 321,818 vs 321,815 (3 lines in 322k), bandwidth 9.273 vs
9.270 GB/s. Two readings are consistent with this, and the reference runs cannot
separate them:

1. **H2 has nothing left to do.** Under WB at A1 the array is already resident
   and WB is not thrashing — 321,818 fills is 1.23 passes' worth, against
   1,314,720 (5.0 passes) at A0. H2's function is to stop a stream from evicting
   itself and its neighbours; where the stream fits, there is nothing to stop.
   Note that H2's own fill count is nearly the same at both points (298,569 at
   A0, 321,815 at A1) — it is the WB arm that moves.
2. **The declaration is not reaching the HNF at A1.** If H2 were excluding, the
   later passes would miss and CXL traffic would rise to ~4 passes. It stays at
   1.00.

Reading 1 explains the fill counts; reading 2 explains the CXL counts; neither
explains both. Deciding between them needs a cell that these reference runs do
not contain. **This is recorded as unresolved and must not be reported as
"H2 is inert at A1" or as "A1 confirms H2 is unnecessary."**

## What this does and does not invalidate

**Does not invalidate the A1 morsel cells now running.** Morsel adds the 8 MiB
hot table: working set 16 MiB fact + 8 MiB hot = 24 MiB against a 20 MiB
effective LLC, a 1.2x overflow. That is not the degenerate fit the reference
runs show, but it is far closer to the boundary than A0's 20 MiB against 5 MiB
(4.0x). The A1 cells will measure something; what they measure is a
mildly-overflowing hierarchy, not the "realistic hierarchy" §2 describes.

**Does invalidate two of the pre-registered readings if taken at face value.**
P1 (A1/B0) and P3 (A1/B1) are both evaluated at the A1 point. Their thresholds
were set for a 32 MiB LLC at 64x the private L2 with the victim at 25% of it.
The realized point is 20 MiB, 40x, victim at 40%.

**Registered now, before the data:** if P1 or P3 is *falsified* at the realized
A1 point, that outcome may not be reported as falsifying the pre-registered
prediction, because the point measured is not the point registered. It may be
reported as what it is — a null at a 20 MiB / 40x / 40% hierarchy — and the
registered prediction stays open. If P1 or P3 is *confirmed*, it is confirmed at
the realized point and must be stated with that point per §5.1, not as
confirmation at "LLC 32 MiB, 64x."

That asymmetry is deliberate and is the conservative direction: a null at the
wrong point is not evidence against the hypothesis, while a positive at the
wrong point is still a positive, provided it is labelled.

**The campaign was not touched.** Twenty-four cells are mid-flight. Per the
standing apparatus rule, apparatus is not changed mid-campaign beyond the
minimum required to run at all, and nothing here prevents these cells from
running. Killing and relaunching A1 is a separate decision that should be made
against the amended geometry, not against this memo.

## The corrected A1, if it is re-run

Two independent problems, and fixing one does not fix the other.

**Geometry.** Pick from the three attainable options above. 40 MiB @ 20-way
keeps the pinned associativity and gives 80x; 32 MiB @ 16-way gives the
registered 64x but moves a parameter the campaign header pins.

**Fact-array size.** The deeper issue is that Knob A raised the LLC 6.4x
nominally while the fact array stayed at 16 MiB, so a stream that overflows A0's
LLC by 3.2x fits inside A1's. Holding the A0 fact:LLC ratio against a 20 MiB
effective LLC needs a **64 MiB** fact array; against 40 MiB, **128 MiB**.

Cost, anchored on measurement rather than estimate. The four completed
references each ran 4 passes (1 warmup + 3 reps) over 16 MiB in 2,256-2,270 s
host, i.e. **~0.157 h per 16 MiB pass**, and the four agree to within 0.6% across
both hierarchies and both policies, so the rate is stable. A 4-pass 64 MiB
reference is therefore ~2.5 h and a 128 MiB one ~5.0 h. Dropping `--reps` from 3
to 1 halves the passes and roughly halves that; the variance estimate is
unaffected because W1.3's method is three repeated *runs*, not repetitions inside
one run. The morsel cells' cost is not yet known — none has finished — so no
morsel re-run estimate is given here.

The scaling rule is a ratio taken from A0, the point that already works, and is
not chosen against any A1 outcome. Whether to spend the hours is not an analysis
decision.

## Addendum, 2026-08-24 (later the same day) — Finding 3 is resolved, and one number above is wrong

Original text left verbatim. Zero further compute: the same four completed
reference runs, one counter that was not being read.

**The wrong number.** Finding 3 reports "HNF fills 321,818 vs 321,815 (3 lines
in 322k)" and concludes the two arms are indistinguishable at the HNF. Fills
*are* those values and they *are* three apart, but fills are the wrong counter to
have stopped at. `hnf.cntrl.cache.numDataArrayWrites` counts **all** data-array
writes at the HNF, and it is not indistinguishable at all:

| cell | HNF data-array writes | of which fills | fills as % of writes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A0 SMOKE wb | 1,315,975 | 1,314,720 | 99.9% |
| A0 SMOKE stream | 298,625 | 298,569 | 100.0% |
| A1 SMOKE wb | 1,475,801 | 321,818 | **21.8%** |
| A1 SMOKE stream | **434,160** | 321,815 | 74.1% |

**H2 removes 70.6% of A1's HNF data-array writes** (1,475,801 -> 434,160), and
77.3% of A0's. **Reading 2 of Finding 3 — "the declaration is not reaching the
HNF at A1" — is refuted.** The declaration reaches the HNF and does the same
proportional work at both hierarchies. Reading 1 stands, and can now be stated
as a mechanism rather than a hypothesis.

**What the removed writes are.** At A0, 99.9% of HNF data-array writes are
fills: the 5 MiB LLC does not hold the 16 MiB array, so a line arriving from the
L2 or from CXL allocates. At A1, only 21.8% are fills — the line is already
resident in the 20 MiB LLC, so the 512 KiB L2's clean evictions update it **in
place** instead of allocating. Non-fill writes at A1/wb are 1,153,983, which is
4.40 passes over the array's 262,144 lines: the thrashing L2 pushes each line
back roughly once per pass. H2 excludes the stream, those evictions stop
carrying data to the HNF, and 1,041,641 of those in-place writes disappear.

**Why nothing reaches memory.** The HNF serves passes 2-4 out of residency, and
the counters say so directly. At A1/wb the HNF sees 1,228,105 prefetch accesses
(4.69 passes) of which 283,582 miss (1.08 passes) and 257,638 demand accesses
(0.98 passes) of which 5,217 miss. Total HNF misses are 288,799 lines = 18.5 MB
against 16.78 MB of measured CXL read — one compulsory pass, as Finding 2 says.
At A0/wb the same sum is 1,346,416 lines = 86.2 MB against 84.40 MB measured.
The accounting closes at both points.

**The corrected Finding 3.** H2's mechanism is not weaker at A1; the hierarchy
is. The fractional reduction in HNF data-array writes is nearly the same at both
points (−77.3% at A0, −70.6% at A1). What differs is what those writes *were*:

- At A0 they are fills, so removing them removes CXL refetches — 84.40 MB ->
  65.21 MB, 5.03 -> 3.89 passes — and buys **16.4% bandwidth** (3.960 -> 4.609
  GB/s) and an LLC demand hit rate of 0.27% -> 27.03%.
- At A1 they are in-place updates to a line that never leaves, so removing them
  changes no fill, no miss, no CXL byte, and no bandwidth (9.273 -> 9.270 GB/s,
  −0.03%). The saving is array-write traffic and LLC write port pressure, which
  this campaign does not price.

This is a sharper statement of the same conclusion the memo already reaches, and
it is the more useful one for the paper: it separates *H2 is inert* — false —
from *H2's effect does not reach memory at this hierarchy* — true, and true for
a reason that is the whole point of Knob A. It does not rehabilitate A1: 9.27
GB/s is still an on-chip number and still cannot be P2's denominator, and P1 and
P3 are still evaluated at a 20 MiB / 40x / 40% point rather than the registered
one. The pre-registered reading above is unchanged.

**Analyzer change, committed with this addendum.** `w7_analyze.py` now reads
`hnf.cntrl.cache.numDataArrayWrites` alongside fills, prints both for every cell
and prints the fill fraction for each stream-smoke reference, so a future reader
cannot repeat the mistake this addendum corrects. The docstring records why the
two counters diverge.

**How the error happened, for the ledger.** The memo read one counter — fills —
found it invariant, and wrote the invariance up as unresolved between two
readings rather than looking for a counter that could separate them. Recording
the ambiguity honestly was right; stopping there was not, because the
discriminating counter was in the same file and cost nothing to read. The
general form: **when a memo says two readings cannot be separated, the next step
is to check whether that is a property of the experiment or only of the columns
that were parsed.** Here it was the columns.
