## The caveat I published this morning is wrong

`tab:fused`'s caption and the co-author cover note (both written today) assert
that the +19--44% restriction penalty is specific to the 50% hit rate and that
the same class of restriction costs 7.5% at a 100% hit rate. **A direct sweep of
exactly that variable says the penalty is largest at a 100% hit rate.** The
caveat is not softened, it is withdrawn.

The evidence I based it on --- M6 pass A's +7.5% at hit rate 1.0 --- is now the
anomaly. Note that M6's mask was *narrower* (2 of 20 ways) and still cost less,
which is backwards on its face and cannot be a hit-rate effect. The two
experiments differ in three other things: fact size (256 MiB vs 1 GiB),
`--reps`/`--warmups` (4/1 vs 1/2), and the resctrl helper (`setup_c` with a
2-way group plus a complement group for the victim CPU, vs `setup_b` with a
single 4-way group). That reconciliation is deliberately not attempted here.

## P2, and an error in my own pre-registration

P2 failed by 0.005 on a window I chose. Both arms individually reproduce
`tab:fused` to within 3%: `none` 89.33 vs 88.45 (+1.0%), `b4` 123.15 vs 126.86
(-2.9%). I registered the ratio window as +/-0.05 absolute (+/-3.5%) while
allowing the instrument check +/-5% on the same arm --- an inconsistency I did not
notice when writing it.

Per the registered rule ("if the instrument check or P2 fails --- void"), **the
e2e gate is not drawn from this run.** I am recording that the gate's direction
was GO (R(0.9) = 1.464 >= 1.25), and that the reason it is not drawn is a
hairline miss on a self-imposed window rather than a substantive failure of arm
identity. Honouring a registration I set badly is the whole point of setting it
in advance; re-reading the window after seeing the number is not available to me.
The e2e decision therefore stays open, and M8 re-registers the same control at
+/-5% for both checks.

## Why M8 exists

The shape of this table raised a question the sweep was not designed to answer.
mos181's LLC is 20 ways x 16 MiB = **320 MiB**; `tab:fused`'s hot table is
`HOT_BYTES = 177838489` = **169.6 MiB**; four of twenty ways is **64 MiB**. So
the `b4` arm does not only restrict the fused class --- it moves the hot table
from a mask it fits inside to one it cannot. A single-class workload with a
169.6 MiB working set would suffer from a 64 MiB mask with no stream present.

If that is the mechanism, `tab:fused`'s 1.43x is a property of the table size we
chose rather than evidence about label scope, and §3's argument that "any mask
narrow enough to constrain the stream constrains the reused structure with it" is
being carried by a number that would appear without a stream. Registered and
running as `M8_TABLEFIT_PREREG_2026-08-26.md`.

## Standing

M7 stands as a measurement: the restriction penalty on `tab:fused`'s arm is
1.16--1.47 across the full hit-rate range and does **not** collapse at realistic
hit rates. Whatever M8 concludes about *why*, the hit-rate caveat comes out of
the paper.
