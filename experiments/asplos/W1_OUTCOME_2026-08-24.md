## What this overturns

`PLAN_B_REBUILD.md` opens with four facts. Two of them are now wrong as stated,
and they are the two the whole Plan B framing rests on.

**Premise 1 — "H2 is inert in our own simulator."** False as a general claim.
H2 is inert *against the finite-SF back-invalidation charge* and removes *90.9%
of the capacity charge*. The inertness was an arm-specific result read as a
mechanism-wide one — which is precisely the failure mode §5.1's arm-identity
rule exists to prevent, and this project committed it against itself.

**Premise 3 — "the two instruments do not meet."** False. The charge silicon
levies on Intel is a **capacity** charge (W5.3: CAT recovers it on mos182). The
charge H2 removes here is a **capacity** charge, at an infinite SF where no
back-invalidation exists by construction. Same charge, two fidelities. That is
the division of labour the paper claimed and could not previously support.

**Premise 2 stands** — H3's back-invalidation charge is still absent on shipping
silicon (W3.1, closed negative), so H3 remains a bounded capability claim.
**Premise 4 stands** — the realised end-to-end magnitudes are still small.

The rebuilt argument in Plan B survives intact and gains its missing half: §"The
rebuilt argument" says "what is missing is (a) evidence that H2 recovers the
charge silicon actually levies, and (b) a cost argument." (a) is this document.
(b) is `W6_COST_ARGUMENT_2026-08-23.md`.

## Mechanism, and the de-confound answered in place

The obvious reviewer objection to any recovery result is that the aggressor
merely got slower. It did not.

| quantity | WB | H2 | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| victim L2 demand misses | 714,210 | 714,068 | **−0.02%** |
| aggressor L2 demand misses **per cycle** | 0.011370 | 0.010928 | **−3.9%** |
| LLC accesses (`ReadShared`) per cycle | 0.036459 | 0.042307 | **+16.0%** |
| off-chip reads (`SendReadNoSnp`) per cycle | 0.030282 | 0.025419 | −16.1% |
| HNF demand misses per cycle | 0.013070 | 0.009973 | −23.7% |
| victim cycles (fixed 3e6 iterations) | 139,140,052 | 105,074,034 | −24.5% |

Three things follow.

1. **The victim's private cache is untouched.** Its L2 demand miss count is
   714k in every arm including quiescent, varying by 0.02%. The entire charge is
   levied below L2, so this is an LLC-level effect and not a private-cache one.
   §5.2's requirement — check the victim's hot set against the private L2 before
   believing a result — is satisfied here in the affirmative direction.
2. **The aggressor is not throttled.** Its L2 misses fall 27% in absolute terms
   only because the victim finishes 24.5% sooner and the run ends. Per cycle it
   pushes within 3.9% of the same private-cache miss rate. The recovery is not
   bought by slowing the streamer. (This is a *bandwidth-matched* de-confound for
   this one cell; W2.1's designed sweep is still owed, and this does not replace
   it.)
3. **The contract is doing exactly what it says.** Under H2 the LLC is *accessed
   16% more per cycle* and goes *off-chip 16% less per cycle*. The stream's
   lookups still happen; the stream's fills do not; the victim stays resident.

Per victim L2 miss, the extra cost falls from **52.50 cycles (WB) to 4.80
(H2)** — a 90.9% reduction, which is the same 90.9% by an independent route.

## Why H2 is inert under a finite SF but not here

The two charges are not additive, and the SF charge subsumes the capacity one.

Going from infinite to finite SF adds **1.132x** of tax under WB and **1.478x**
under H2. H2 makes the SF-added charge slightly *worse*, and that is expected:
the snoop filter tracks **private-cache** contents, which H2 by construction does
not touch — keeping streaming lines out of the LLC does nothing about the
streaming lines sitting in the aggressor's 2 MiB L2. Excluding the stream from
the LLC while the SF still thrashes leaves the victim's lines being
back-invalidated out of its private cache regardless of whether they are LLC
resident. Removing LLC pressure buys nothing when the binding constraint is
elsewhere.

That is the whole reconciliation, and it makes W3.1's closure load-bearing rather
than merely prudent: **shipping snoop filters do not thrash** (mos182 stays at
1.020x with 62 MiB of streaming private footprint, bounded at 1.0998x; gem5's SF
sits at 1.0x coverage of aggregate private L2 where shipping parts sit at a
multiple). So on the parts the paper targets, the SF charge is not the binding
one, the capacity charge is, and H2 removes 90.9% of it.

## One anomaly, flagged and not resolved

The HNF demand-hit identity holds exactly in both arms
(hits + misses = victim L2 misses + aggressor L2 misses), so nothing is
miscounted. But under H2 the HNF serves **814,389** demand hits against a victim
that only makes 714,068 demand accesses. At least **100,321** aggressor demand
accesses — 8.7% of them — hit in an LLC that H2 is supposed to be excluding them
from. Under WB the same subtraction is negative, so it cannot be read off there.

Two candidate explanations, untested: the aggressor's non-stream footprint
(stack, code, control structures) is not marked streaming and legitimately
belongs in the LLC; or the bypass is incomplete on some path. The first is the
more likely and would account for a single-digit percentage. It does not affect
the verdict — an incomplete bypass would *understate* H2 — but it is a
mechanism-fidelity question and it should be answered before the number is
published, because a reviewer can do this subtraction too.

## What is now unblocked, and what is not

- **W2 is unblocked.** W2.1 (bandwidth-matched de-confound) now has both a
  target and a partial answer; W2.2 (WSS sensitivity) and W2.3 (LLC
  size/associativity) turn a single point into a curve.
- **The paper's benefit claim has a defensible home**: H2, at an infinite SF,
  against a capacity charge, on a victim whose private cache is provably not
  involved.
- **Still gated:** W1.2's consistency prediction for H2+H3/infinite, registered
  2026-08-23 23:41 before those runs started — same as or slightly worse than
  H2/infinite, ±5% counts as the same, >10% better means the `tab:h3sf` H3
  attribution must be withdrawn. Not yet evaluable.
- **Unchanged:** H3 stays a bounded capability claim (W3.1). W7's convergence
  problem is untouched by this — W7 is about a workload that both *requires*
  object scope and is *fixed* by it, and this victim requires nothing.
- **Not unblocked:** every paper edit remains held pending the lead's decision on
  co-author communication. This result changes what the edit queue should say —
  see below — but not whether it can be applied.
