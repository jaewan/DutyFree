## P2 holds on the comparison it was meant to make --- my binning was wrong

At **matched** occupancy, way count does not matter:

| occupancy | 5 ways | 10 ways | 14 ways | spread |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 40% | +0.0% | +0.0% | −0.0% (43%) | 0.0 pts |
| 80% | +0.3% | +0.2% | +0.3% (86%) | 0.1 pts |
| 120% | +55.0% | +57.1% | --- | **2.1 pts** |

All three matched comparisons agree to within 2.1 points across a 2.8x range of
way counts. **Occupancy is the right variable within a geometry.**

The analyzer reports P2 as failing only because I defined a `150--300%` band that
lumps 160% and 240% occupancy together --- two *different* occupancies, which of
course differ (+118.8% vs +181.5%). That is a defect in my binning, not in the
data, and it is the **seventh** specification error of this campaign: previous six
were thresholds set below instrument resolution, this one is a comparison defined
so that a real effect registers as a failure. Recorded rather than silently
re-binned.

One cell is **unresolved** per the registered CoV gate: 5 ways at 120%, CoV 7.72%
against a 0.66% basis --- the cell nearest the knee, and the same bimodal signature
mos181's masked arms showed. Its neighbour at the same occupancy (10 ways, CoV
0.34%) carries the comparison.

## What this does to E1's rule

**The form survives; the coefficient does not.**

- **Survives:** confinement is free while a party's partition is comfortably
  larger than its working set, and expensive once it is not; the transition is
  sharp; and within a geometry the cost is a function of occupancy alone, not of
  way count. Two geometries, 26 cells, one shape.
- **Does not survive:** *where* the transition falls. It is at ~2/3 on mos181 and
  at ~1.0 on mos182, so **"tenant WS + neighbour WS $\lesssim$ 2/3 x LLC" must not
  be written as a law.**
- **Restated honestly:** a free static split exists only if the two parties'
  combined working sets fit the cache **with platform-specific headroom**, which
  we measure as $c \approx 2/3$ on a 320 MiB / 20-way part and $c \approx 1.0$ on
  a 60 MiB / 15-way part. **On both parts, the configurations we care about fail
  the condition** --- mos181 needs 439 MiB of 320; mos182 needs 64 MiB of 60 at
  pass B's sizes --- so E1's *conclusion* stands on both hosts while its
  *coefficient* does not generalise.

That is a weaker claim than E1 stated and a stronger one than a single measured
point, and it is the version the paper can defend.

## Candidate mechanisms for the shift, none demonstrated

- **Absolute footprint and page-walk pressure.** mos181's victim is 3.4x larger
  (162 vs 48 MiB), i.e. ~41,500 vs ~12,300 4 KiB pages. Way-restriction may
  interact with the page walker's own footprint, which scales with the victim and
  not with the partition.
- **Way size.** 16 MiB per way against 4 MiB changes how a random walk's lines
  distribute across sets at a given occupancy.
- **LLC inclusivity or replacement policy** differing between EMR and SPR.

Distinguishing these needs a footprint-matched comparison across hosts --- the same
absolute victim working set on both --- which is cheap and is the obvious follow-up.
Not run.

## What this cannot show

Two Intel server geometries, both way-partitioned, both with a 2 MiB private L2.
It tests transfer across cache size and way size, **not** across microarchitecture
family, replacement policy, or inclusivity. AMD is untested and its harm is
rate-class rather than capacity-class. One victim type on both hosts, so
victim-type generality --- the other option the agenda offered --- remains open.

# Pass B: the trade-off does not travel, and on mos182 partitioning strictly dominates

n=6, **30/30 records**, tenant liveness asserted **18/18**, CoV 0.01--0.65%,
resctrl clean. Preflight passed (see the void attempt below).

| arm | victim cyc/load | vs alone | CoV |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| victim alone | 79.442 | 1.000 | 0.01% |
| victim confined to 10 of 15 ways, **no tenant** | 79.621 | **1.002** | 0.02% |
| + tenant, unmasked, stream retained | 189.894 | 2.390 | 0.34% |
| + tenant, unmasked, **stream non-allocating** | 152.108 | **1.915** | 0.65% |
| + tenant confined to 5 ways | 78.618 | **0.990** | 0.02% |

- **P3a FAILS.** The label removes **34.2%** of the harm, against a registered
  >= 50%. On mos181 it removed 75%.
- **P3b HOLDS.** The mask returns the victim to **0.9874x** of its own confined
  baseline, and that baseline costs only **+0.2%**.
- **P4 holds trivially**: combined working sets are 64 MiB against a 60 MiB LLC
  (107%), so no split can be cheap for both, on either platform's coefficient.

## P3a's failure is mostly my design, and I disclosed the defect while registering it

The two-component account predicts the label's protection falls as the *tenant's*
footprint grows as a fraction of the LLC. Against mos181's own curve (E2B):

| tenant footprint, as % of LLC | 1.2% | 5% | 20% | **40%** | 53% | **80%** |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| harm removed, mos181 | 100.7 | 100.7 | 96.6 | **74.1** | --- | **14.3** |
| harm removed, mos182 | | | | | **34.2** | |

53% sits between mos181's 40% and 80% points, and 34.2% is inside that convex
interval. **The account travels.** What did not travel is my scaling:

| | mos181 (M12) | mos182 (E3b) |
|---|--:|--:|
| victim, as % of LLC | 51% | **53%** --- matched |
| tenant table, as % of LLC | 40% | **53%** --- *not* matched |

The pre-registration states this in the design section --- *"32 MiB table ... 53% of
this LLC, against 128 MiB = 40% on mos181"* --- and I set P3a's threshold anyway.
**Eighth specification defect: a threshold calibrated for a configuration the
design did not deliver, with the mismatch written down one paragraph earlier.**
A matched test needs a 24 MiB tenant table (40% of 60 MiB), which is not a power of
two times 16 B and would round --- the honest fix is 16 MiB (27%) and accepting a
different comparison point, or re-running mos181 at 53%.

## The finding that *is* geometry, and it is the load-bearing one

| | mos181 | mos182 |
|---|--:|--:|
| victim's confinement cost at ~80% occupancy | **+10.8%** | **+0.2%** |
| mask's protection vs the victim's own confined baseline | 0.992x | 0.987x |

**On mos182, partitioning is nearly free to the neighbour and fully protective, so
it strictly dominates the label there.** This is not a size artifact --- it follows
directly from pass A's knee: the victim at 80% occupancy sits *below* mos182's
~100% knee and *above* mos181's ~2/3 one.

So the paper's central trade-off table is **mos181-specific, and for a reason we
now understand**: the trade-off is only non-trivial where the neighbour's
confinement cost is material, and that cost is a function of where the platform's
knee falls. On a part whose knee is at 100%, a neighbour with room to spare pays
nothing to be partitioned, and there is no trade-off to report.

**Consequence for the paper.** The trade-off table must carry its platform and its
mechanism: *"on a part where the neighbour's working set sits above the
confinement knee, partitioning charges it 13.1% and the label charges nothing; on
a part where it sits below, partitioning is free and dominates."* That is a
narrower claim than the current table implies, and it is the one E3 supports.

## The void first attempt, recorded

The first run of pass B produced 24 records in which **every co-run arm measured
the victim alone.** The tenant binary on mos182 predated `--flush-distance` and
exited with `unknown argument`; the liveness assertion caught it in flight
(`alive_at_end: 0, warmed: 0`) and 14 warnings were emitted. This is the same
failure class as M1's positive control, which went undetected at the time.

Fixes applied: data quarantined as `e3b_VOID_stale_binary_*.jsonl`; the tenant
rebuilt on mos182 from source md5-identical to mos181's (`59e5bec5`); and a
**preflight added that exercises every flag the arms use and aborts unless the
benchmark returns `status: ok`**, so a stale binary cannot consume a campaign.

One caveat that cannot be fixed, only stated: the Makefile uses `-march=native`,
so the two hosts run **different binaries** (`d2163128` vs `d60871439`) built from
identical source with identical flags. That is the best available for a cross-host
comparison and it is a caveat, not a non-issue.
