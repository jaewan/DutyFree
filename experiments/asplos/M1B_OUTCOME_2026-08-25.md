# M1b + diagnostic: the three-party premise HOLDS, and its remedy FAILS

## First: M1 is void, and not for the reason M1 said

M1 reported its instrument falsifier firing (control 0.9649×, needing ≥1.50×) and
concluded the arms were void. **The falsifier verdict was itself an artifact.**

A diagnostic on a quiet machine (`diag_victim_baseline.sh`), victim identical in
every parameter:

| step | victim cyc/load | HugePages_Free |
|---|--:|--:|
| cold #1 / #2 / #3 | **78.095 / 78.081 / 78.081** | 34288 |
| with fused tenant | 212.251 | 34288 |
| after fused, +0 s | **78.082** | 34288 |
| with 23.3 GB/s streamer | 164.564 | 32240 |
| after streamer, +0 s | **78.082** | 34288 |
| after streamer, +5 s | 78.087 | 34288 |

V-alone is **78.08**, reproducibly, and recovers **immediately** — no hysteresis.
M1's V-alone of 201.332 is therefore not a baseline; it was measured under load.

**The cause was mine.** Between M1's two discarded attempts I killed the runner,
`pointer_chase` and `stream_wb` — but never `cxl_join_bench`, whose co-runners
were launched with `--reps 2000` (~206 s at 16 threads on cores 32–47). Leftover
fused tenants were still on the machine. Every M1 arm, *including its baseline*,
was measured against that background, which is exactly why every M1 harm ratio
compressed toward 1.0 and why a 23.3 GB/s streamer appeared to cost 2.8%.

M1's own numbers are consistent with this: its "V alone" (201.3) sits almost
exactly where the diagnostic puts *V under a fused tenant* (212.3).

**M1 is withdrawn in full** — its falsifier verdict, its primary reading, and its
"harm is flat in worker count" secondary. The flatness was saturation of an
already-saturated victim, not a property of fused tenants. M1b's baseline
(78.088) matches the diagnostic to 0.01, so M1b stands.

## The corrected picture

| co-runner | victim cyc/load | harm |
|---|--:|--:|
| none | 78.08 | 1.00× |
| dedicated streamer, 23.3 GB/s | 164.56 | **2.11×** |
| fused tenant, 16 workers | 209.05 – 212.25 | **2.68 – 2.72×** |

The positive control passes comfortably. **A fused tenant harms a neighbour
2.7×** — more than a dedicated stream at 4.4× its bandwidth. The panel's
three-party premise **holds**, and holds more strongly than they argued for.

## M1b: which component of F does it? Not the stream.

n=6 per arm, position-balanced, CoV 0.02–0.73%:

| arm | cyc/load | harm | vs `V+F_big` |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| V | 78.088 | 1.000× | — |
| **V+F_big** (256 MiB stream + 256 MiB table) | 209.051 | 2.677× | 1.0000 |
| **V+F_notstream** (no stream, 256 MiB table) | 209.281 | 2.680× | **1.0011** |
| **V+F_small** (256 MiB stream, 4 MiB table) | 182.421 | 2.336× | 0.8726 |

The two right-hand ratios need no baseline and so survive independently of the
whole M1 mess:

- **Removing F's entire 256 MiB CXL stream changes the neighbour's cost by
  0.11%.**
- Shrinking F's table 64× removes only **12.7%** of the harm.

The arms are strongly **sub-additive**: either component alone reproduces nearly
all of the harm. That is saturation — V's 170 MB is LLC-resident at 78 cyc/load
and DRAM-bound at ~209, and essentially any substantial co-runner flips it. Once
flipped, more pressure adds nothing.

## What this does to the three-party reframing

**Stakeholder: confirmed.** A fused tenant really does harm a latency-critical
neighbour, at 2.7×, which is what the frame needed and what M1 appeared to deny.

**Remedy: refuted.** The frame's remedy is a *stream-scoped* label — H2 on F's
fact stream. F's stream contributes **0.11%** of the harm. Removing it entirely
does nothing for V. A stream-scoped mechanism cannot protect V from F.

What does harm V is F occupying the shared cache at all — its 256 MiB hot table
(13%) plus, dominantly, the saturating effect of sixteen workers touching memory.
F's hot table is F's own working data and must stay write-back; no
`Streaming`-style declaration applies to it.

So the middle row of the panel's table gains a stakeholder and loses its remedy.
The honest version of that row is:

| | harm to V | deployed remedy | remedy's price | label's advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V vs. fused tenant | **2.7× (measured)** | CAT(+MBA) on F's class | +19–44% on F's reuse structure (`tab:fused`) | **none — the label would scope the stream, and the stream is 0.11% of the harm** |

CAT still works here, and still charges F's reuse structure for it. That is the
expressibility argument intact — but the *advantage* column, which was the whole
point of proposing a page-scoped type, is empty in this configuration.

## Caveats, registered and observed

- The pre-registered thresholds returned "mixed; report the split", and that is
  what is reported. Neither "the table does it" nor "the stream does it" is
  claimed.
- **Saturation limits what this geometry can resolve.** V is either LLC-resident
  or not; the metric is close to binary here. Distinguishing *degrees* of
  pressure needs a victim that is not driven straight to DRAM by any co-runner —
  a smaller victim, a larger LLC share, or a partitioned baseline.
- `V+F_small` runs F's probes faster, so it streams *more* per unit time. That
  biases toward finding a stream effect, so the 0.11% null is conservative.
- Everything here is mos181 (EMR, 320 MiB LLC, non-inclusive L3). AMD is
  unavailable; broker has refused every SSH handshake for two days.
