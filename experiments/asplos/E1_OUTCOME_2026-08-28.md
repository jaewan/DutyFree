# E1 outcome: P1 holds. No static split satisfies both parties, and the condition is derivable.

Pre-registration `E1_FRONTIER_PREREG_2026-08-28.md` (`147f743`), runners and
analyzer committed before their runs. mos181. Pass A n=10 (60 runs), pass B n=6
(**288/288 records**, all 48 cells), tenant liveness asserted in **180/180**
co-run records, zero warnings, resctrl clean. Instrument check **PASSES**: victim
unconfined at 170 MB = 78.042, inside the registered [75.72, 80.40].

## The frontier

$C_T$ = tenant's own cost at $k$ ways. $C_V$ = victim's own confinement cost at
$20-k$ ways, no tenant. $H$ = victim's harm while the tenant runs, **against its
own confined baseline**.

| victim WSS | tenant ways | victim ways | victim partition full | $C_V$ | $C_T$ | $H$ retain | $H$ flush |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 170 MB | 2 | 18 | 59% | +0.1% | +32.7% | 0.988 | 0.988 |
| 170 MB | 4 | 16 | 66% | +0.4% | +24.7% | 0.988 | 0.989 |
| 170 MB | 8 | 12 | 89% | **+13.1%** | +8.7% | 0.989 | 0.988 |
| 170 MB | 12 | 8 | 133% | +65.2% | +1.3% | 0.989 | 0.989 |
| 170 MB | 16 | 4 | 266% | +137.7% | +0.0% | 0.993 | 0.994 |
| 96 MB | 12 | 8 | 75% | +10.8% | +1.3% | 0.988 | 0.988 |
| 96 MB | 16 | 4 | 150% | +84.4% | +0.0% | 0.992 | 0.991 |
| 32 MB | 16 | 4 | 50% | +0.2% | +0.0% | 0.989 | 0.988 |

(Full 15-cell matrix in `benchmarks/data/e1b_frontier/`.)

## Result 1 --- P1 holds: the cheap regions are disjoint

At the 170 MB victim, the tenant is cheap ($C_T \le 10\%$) **only at $k \ge 8$**
and the victim is cheap ($C_V \le 5\%$) **only at $k \le 4$**. No split satisfies
both. **P2, the collapse condition, does not fire.**

The decision rests on the well-resolved axis: victim resolution is ~1.1% at n=6
and the binding number is 13.1%, about 12x that. (Pass A's tenant CoV reached
7.07% at narrow masks against the 3.7% my power calc assumed, so the tenant-side
threshold was only ~1.1x resolution --- recorded below --- but no split is decided
on the tenant gate alone.)

## Result 2 --- partitioning *always* protects perfectly. The cost is the whole story.

$H$ is **0.988--0.994 at every split and every victim working set.** Once the
victim holds an enforced partition, the co-running tenant costs it nothing
measurable, whether the tenant has 2 ways or 16, and whether its stream allocates
or not. (The consistent 1% *below* unity is the uncore-frequency systematic
identified in M12 --- 16 extra active cores --- not the tenant helping the victim.)

So way-partitioning is not a partial mitigation whose residual we are measuring.
**It is a complete mitigation whose entire price is the confinement it imposes**,
and the only question a static split answers is *which party pays*.

## Result 3 --- the confinement cost is a clean function of partition occupancy

Pooling all 15 cells, $C_V$ against how full the victim's own partition is:

| partition full | 11--66% | 75% | 89% | 133% | 150% | 266% |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| $C_V$ | **−0.0 to +0.4%** | +10.8% | +13.1% | +65.2% | +84.4% | +137.7% |

**P3 HOLDS.** Free below ~2/3 full, expensive above ~3/4, and the knee sits
between 66% and 75%. Fifteen cells across three working sets and five way counts
collapse onto one curve.

### The interim claim I made from n=3 was noise, and I retract it

Two turns ago I reported that "the cost is not pure occupancy --- 4 ways hurts at
50% full while 12 ways does not", from a cell reading **+7.2%** at n=3. At n=6
that same cell reads **+0.2%**. There is no independent associativity effect in
this data; occupancy explains all of it. I flagged the interim numbers as
provisional and they were, but the claim should not have been stated as a
mechanism.

## Result 4 --- the free-split condition, derived and predictive

Because each party is free while its partition is $\lesssim 66\%$ full, a free
static split exists **iff**

$$\text{tenant working set} + \text{neighbour working set} \;\lesssim\; 0.66 \times \text{LLC}$$

Here: the tenant needs $128/0.66 = 194$ MiB and the victim needs $162/0.66 = 245$
MiB, a combined demand of **439 MiB against a 320 MiB cache**. The two working
sets alone are 290 MiB, **91% of the LLC**, already past the threshold before any
headroom.

This is the strongest form of the claim and it is *quantitative and testable*
rather than a single measured point: **partitioning cannot protect a neighbour
from a streaming tenant without charging one of them, whenever their combined
working sets exceed about two thirds of the shared cache.** An object label
removes the stream's demand without partitioning at all, so it is not subject to
the inequality --- which is the reason it charges neither party.

## P4 fails, and it puts every tenant-cost number in the paper in question

At the shared point ($k=8$, 170 MB) E1 measures $C_T = +8.7\%$ where M12 measured
**+16.7%**. Same table (128 MiB), same stream (1 GiB), same hit rate, same
`--reps 20`, same cores. The one difference is the resctrl helper:

- **M12 used `setup_b`**: the tenant gets 8 ways and the **root group keeps the
  full 20-way mask**, so every other CPU --- kernel work, interrupts, an idle
  cpu 8 --- can still allocate inside the tenant's 8 ways.
- **E1 uses `setup_c`**: the complement is enforced to cpu 8, so nothing else
  allocates in the tenant's ways.

This reproduces the direction M11b saw and could not resolve (p = 0.443 at
+/-14 pp); at n=10 with CoV 3.27% the two differ by ~4 SE, so it is now real.

**Consequence, per the registered rule (quarantine and reconcile).** Every
tenant-cost figure currently in the paper --- §5's 16.7%, the abstract's 17--41%,
`tab:catmba`'s range, and M10/M10b's whole sweep --- was measured with `setup_b`,
i.e. with background allocation permitted inside the tenant's mask. If `setup_c`
is the right apparatus, **they are all inflated**, and E1's numbers (+32.7% /
+24.7% / +8.7% / +1.3% / +0.0%) supersede them. That reconciliation is the next
experiment and must happen before any of those numbers are written up.

The victim-side numbers are unaffected: pass B used `setup_c` throughout, and
M12's $C_V = 13.1\%$ reproduces exactly here.

## What this cannot show

Intel EMR only; one tenant benchmark; one victim type (a latency-bound pointer
chase, the most sensitive neighbour available); one tenant table; one stream size;
one hit rate. **Static** partitions only --- a dynamically reconfigured mask is a
different mechanism and untested. The 66% knee is measured on this victim and this
cache geometry; whether the coefficient travels is exactly what E3 (second victim,
second host) would test. And the label's "charges neither party" column remains an
idealisation the flush-behind proxy cannot demonstrate on the tenant's axis (M12
pass A).

## The bimodality is real, reproducible, and width-selective

The registered guard fired (CoV > 3% at three widths) and printed every pair, so
this is visible rather than averaged away:

| width | modes | spread | low mode | high mode |
|--:|---|--:|---|---|
| 2 | **bimodal** | 19.0% | 1.3261 (n=5) | 1.5411 (n=5) |
| 4 | **bimodal** | 15.4% | 1.2472 (n=4) | 1.4232 (n=6) |
| 8 | **bimodal** | 7.9% | 1.0880 (n=3) | 1.1713 (n=7) |
| 12 | unimodal | 2.1% | --- | 1.0208 |
| 16 | unimodal | 0.9% | --- | 1.0026 |

The unmasked half of every pair is rock-stable (77.95--78.53 across all 50 runs),
so **the mode switching lives entirely in the masked arm**, and only where the
mask constrains the table (widths 2--8, i.e. 32--128 MiB against a 128 MiB table).
Widths that comfortably hold the table do not switch.

That pattern points at **set-index aliasing**: with 4 KiB pages, a fixed way mask
and a table whose size is at or above the mask's capacity, run-to-run differences
in physical page placement change how the table's lines collide, and the outcome
lands in one of a small number of stable configurations. It is a hypothesis, not a
demonstration --- confirming it needs page-placement control we do not have here.

**What it means for reporting.** Medians across >= 10 pairs reproduce across
independent campaigns to within 0.1% at 8 ways (1.1695 vs 1.1697). Individual runs
do not. So every tenant-cost figure must come from a median over pairs, and the
bimodality must be disclosed wherever the number appears --- it is not noise to be
averaged silently, it is two stable states of the same configuration.

## The 2/3 occupancy knee now has two independent derivations

Pass B gave the victim's knee. Pass A2 gives the tenant's, from a different
workload, a different binary, and a different metric:

| party | free at | occupancy there | cost | binding at | occupancy | cost |
|---|---|--:|--:|---|--:|--:|
| tenant (128 MiB table) | 12 ways = 192 MiB | **67%** | +2.1% | 8 ways = 128 MiB | 100% | +16.9% |
| victim (162 MiB set) | 16 ways = 256 MiB | **63%** | +0.4% | 12 ways = 192 MiB | 84% | +13.1% |

**Both parties obey the same knee, between about two thirds and three quarters
full.** That the coefficient falls out the same way from a 16-thread hash-join
tenant and a single-threaded pointer chase is the strongest thing E1 produced, and
it is what makes the free-split condition a rule rather than a fitted curve:

$$\text{a free static split exists} \iff \text{tenant WS} + \text{neighbour WS} \;\lesssim\; \tfrac{2}{3}\times\text{LLC}$$

Here $128 + 162 = 290$ MiB demands $439$ MiB of partition against a $320$ MiB
cache. No split exists, which is what the frontier shows cell by cell.
