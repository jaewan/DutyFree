# W2.1 — "the aggressor just got slower" is mechanistically excluded

Written 2026-08-24. **Zero compute: re-analysis of the nine W1 infinite-SF runs
(`/tmp/sf_{qui,wb,h2}_inf_s{1,2,3}`), n=3 per arm.**

`PLAN_B_REBUILD.md` W2.1: *"Show H2's recovery is not 'the aggressor got
slower.' Without this, the first reviewer kills the result."* It is listed as
the only genuinely unbuilt item in W2 and was scoped as a new MSHR-throttled
bandwidth band (P0-1). **The band is no longer load-bearing.** The existing runs
already contain the discriminator, and it is a stronger one than a matched
point, because it does not depend on the shape of the victim's response to
bandwidth.

## The measurement

`mem_ctrls0` = 0–128 GiB @ 98 ns = local DRAM, where the victim's 2650 KiB chase
lives. `mem_ctrls1` = 128–256 GiB @ 203 ns = CXL, where the aggressor's 16 MiB
stream lives. The split is address-based and clean: the **victim-alone arm moves
zero bytes on CXL**, so all CXL traffic is the aggressor's.

| | victim alone | WB | H2 | WB→H2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| victim cyc/access | 33.8814 | 46.3800 | 35.0247 | −24.48% |
| **victim L2 demand misses** | 713,948 | **714,210** | **714,068** | **−0.02%** |
| …of which reach DRAM | 201 | **445,535** | **3,205** | **−99.28%** |

| aggressor CXL read GB/s | 0 | 3.2929 | 3.0872 | **−6.25%** |

sd (n=3) on the aggressor's read bandwidth: 0.00084 (WB), 0.00020 (H2) GB/s. The
6.25% drop is ~245 sd. **It is real and it must be reported, not denied.**

## Why the confound is excluded

**The victim issues the same number of L2 misses in both arms — 714,210 vs
714,068, 0.02% apart. What changes is where those misses are served.** Under
write-back, 62.4% of them reach DRAM; under H2, at most 0.45% do (an upper
bound: DRAM traffic under H2 also contains the aggressor's stack and code).

No bandwidth throttle can do that. Slowing a streamer shortens queues and lowers
the *latency* of a miss; it cannot move a miss from DRAM to the LLC. The only
thing that relocates a miss is whether the line was there to be found, and that
is what an admission decision controls. The corroborating protocol counter is
already in W1.4: LLC inbound fills (`LocalHN_Eviction`) fall **4,424,887 →
31,669, −99.3%**, which is the gate closing.

The quantitative version of the same point: under WB the victim is LLC-served on
37.6% of its L2 misses, under H2 on 99.55%. A 6.25% reduction in the streamer's
fill rate does not produce a 37.6 → 99.55 point move in a 5 MiB LLC against a
16 MiB stream. The two arms are not the same experiment at two bandwidths.

The statistical version, for completeness and because it is the weaker one: the
whole bandwidth range 3.29 GB/s → 0 is worth 46.38 → 33.88 cyc/access. If the
victim's response to bandwidth were linear, a 6.25% cut buys **6.9% of the
observed recovery** and 93% is unexplained. Linearity is an assumption and the
assumption-free bound is vacuous — which is precisely why this line of argument
is the *supporting* one here and the miss-relocation result is the load-bearing
one.

## The 6.25% is probably a cost of H2, not a confound

Two facts point the causal arrow away from "the streamer was throttled":

1. **The gate's effect on the streamer does not run through bandwidth.** H2
   closes the fill path by predicate. The aggressor's LLC fill pressure goes to
   ~zero whether it is streaming at 3.3 or 0.3 GB/s. The victim's residency is
   downstream of the gate, not of the rate.
2. **H2 denies the streamer its writeback absorption, and the bill is visible.**
   CXL writes go 1,877 → 1,698,091 bytes. Under write-back the streamer's dirty
   lines land in the LLC (`alloc_on_writeback=True`) and mostly never reach
   memory before the run ends; under H2 they go to CXL at 203 ns. That is
   ~30.7 MB/s of new write traffic the streamer did not previously pay, on the
   far side of the slowest link in the machine.

So the honest reading is that **H2 costs the declared stream about 6% of its
delivered bandwidth**, and the victim's 24.5% recovery is not bought out of it —
it is bought out of residency the stream was destroying and no longer is. Both
halves belong in the paper. The second one is a cost we have measured rather
than assumed, and it is the first streamer-side price tag anywhere in this work.

## What this does and does not close

**Closed:** the reviewer objection as stated. The recovery is not a throttling
artifact, and the argument does not rest on a response-shape assumption.

**Downgraded, not cancelled:** the P0-1 MSHR bandwidth band. It is now a
robustness result rather than the thing the claim stands on. If it is run, the
design constraints from the vetted plan still hold — global L1/L2 MSHR caps (not
`PF_DEGREE_*`, which is global and detunes the victim too), verify victim-alone
`numCycles` is invariant under the cap, and report across a band rather than at
one matched point. **Pre-registered prediction, fixed now:** across a
0.8–3.3 GB/s band the victim's LLC-served fraction stays near 37% in the WB arm
and near 99% in the H2 arm at every point, because the fraction is set by the
gate and not by the rate. If the WB arm's LLC-served fraction climbs toward 99%
as bandwidth falls, the confound is live after all and this memo is wrong.

**Not addressed here:** whether the 6.25% is specific to this streamer's
dirty-line behaviour. A read-only stream with no writebacks would not pay the
absorption penalty and might show a smaller drop. Worth one arm if W2.1 is
revisited.
