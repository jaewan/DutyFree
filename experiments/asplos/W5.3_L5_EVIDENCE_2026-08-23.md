# W5.3 -- The L5 death, converted to a table

Status: drafted as a `~/DutyFree` document. **Nothing has been written to
`~/STREAMING_Paper/`.** Applying it is a S9 lead decision (page-1 evidentiary
posture) and is held until the co-authors have been told.

**This document does not contain new analysis.** The analysis is
`benchmarks/e2e/oltp_index/GPROBE_OUTCOME.md` S5.3-S5.7, which is committed and
which is the authority for every number below. W5.3's job is narrower: turn
that analysis into the single table the paper needs, and write down what the
table may and may not be used to claim. I recomputed every cell independently
from the raw artifacts using medians (GPROBE reports means); the two agree
within 2.2% everywhere and I have kept GPROBE's numbers, not mine, so that the
paper and the outcome document cannot drift.

## The table

| | LLC / ways | probe WS | unmitigated | CAT12 partition cost, no co-runner | **co-run residual under CAT12** | MBA alone | CAT12 + MBA | streamer BW cost of the working mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intel SPR (mos182, 8462Y+) | 60 MiB / 15 | 60 MiB (1.0x LLC) | 1.62x | 1.222x | **1.00x** -- CAT complete | 1.476x | 1.229x | 0.7% (CAT confines streamer to 3 ways: 23.56 vs 23.73 GB/s) |
| AMD Bergamo (moscxl, 9754) | 16 MiB/CCX / 16 | 8 MiB (0.5x L3) | 18.73x | 1.064x | **12.4x** -- CAT fails | 4.13x | 1.07x | 4% |

The load-bearing column is the middle one, and it is the one that needs the
no-co-runner control to be readable at all. On Intel the entire 1.22x that
remains under CAT is what the victim pays for being confined to 48 of 60 MiB
with nobody else on the machine; against that control the co-runner costs it
**nothing measurable**. On AMD the partition costs 6.4% by itself and the
victim is still 12.4x slower than the same partition running alone.

Two vendors, two charges, and each vendor's knob is useless against the other's
charge:

| | harm class | the knob that answers it | the knob that does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intel SPR | capacity | CAT: 1.62x -> co-run residual 1.00x | MBA: -47% streamer BW buys 9% of the excess, and 0% once CAT is on |
| AMD Bergamo | bandwidth / queueing | MBA (with CAT): -> 1.07x at 96% BW | CAT alone: 18.73x -> 13.05x, two thirds of the harm left standing |

## Why the harm classes are measurements, not labels

**Intel = capacity.** `probe_mos182_cat12_control.jsonl` supplies the
denominator the 2x2 was missing: CAT12_nostream reads 1.222x at WS=60 MiB and
CAT12 co-run reads 0.997x against it. A 23.7 GB/s, 8-core, LLC-thrashing
co-runner costs a 12-of-15-way-granted victim nothing, at either working set,
at any MBA setting. MBA looks inert on Intel not because it is a weak knob but
because CAT already took the whole tax.

**AMD = not capacity.** The cleanest cell in the campaign is at WS=256 KB under
MBA8: the streamer holds **15.9 of the 16 MiB L3** and the victim reads
**exactly 1.000x**. Full footprint, no harm. Throttling the request rate does
not reduce the streamer's occupancy -- at 0.94 GB/s over an 8 s arm it still
rewrites 16 MiB many times over, and the occupancy column confirms it did --
yet the harm disappears. Whatever the AMD charge is, it is levied on the rate
and not on the footprint.

## Where the cap has to sit, on AMD

`_knee2` was run specifically to separate "the cap binds" from "arming the
delay-injection logic perturbs the streamer", using settings above what 7 cores
can pull:

| arm (WS = 8 MiB, CAT12 in place) | streamer GB/s | victim tax |
|---|---|---|
| CAT12 + MBA256 (nominal 32 GB/s, does not bind) | 24.52 | 13.35x |
| CAT12 + MBA224 (nominal 28 GB/s, does not bind) | 24.54 | 12.44x |
| CAT12 + MBA192 (nominal 24 GB/s, barely binds) | 23.56 | **1.08x** |
| CAT12 + MBA176 | 20.95 | 1.08x |

Arming MBA without binding it does nothing. Recovery appears exactly where the
cap starts to bind, between 24.5 and 23.6 GB/s delivered, and does not improve
with further throttling (1.08x at 96% of full bandwidth, 1.084x at 8%).

The right reading is *saturation*, not an arbitrary cliff: the cap has to sit
just below where the memory system saturates. That is a physically meaningful
place -- but it is a property of this streamer, this victim, this core count
and this machine, and an operator has to find it. Being 4% too generous buys
nothing at all.

## Reproducibility

Rep-to-rep spread on every cell above is 0.0-5.3%, except `CAT12_MBA224` at 17%
(714.88 / 723.17 / 836.12 cyc/access) -- whose *best* rep is still 11.7x, so
the conclusion does not turn on it. The AMD `CAT12` @ 8 MiB cell was measured in
four independent campaigns on separate days: 13.00x, 13.17x, 13.21x, 13.28x by
median, 2.2% across campaigns.

## Configuration facts that must travel with the table

Three of these are host asymmetries a reviewer will find if the table does not
disclose them.

- **"CAT12" is not the same fraction on the two hosts.** mos182: `FULL = 7fff`,
  15 ways, 60 MiB LLC, so CAT12 = 12/15 = 48 MiB victim, streamer 3 ways
  (`run_probe.py:44`, `run_mba_mos182.py:59`). moscxl: `FULL = ffff`, 16 ways of
  1 MiB per CCX, so CAT12 = 12/16 = 12 MiB victim, streamer 4 MiB
  (`run_probe_moscxl.py:48,55`). This independently confirms W4.3/F7.1: any way
  fraction quoted for c4 work is a fraction of 15, not 20.
- **The working sets are not the same fraction of cache.** Intel 60 MiB = 1.0x
  its LLC; AMD 8 MiB = 0.5x its per-CCX L3. Each row sits at its own host's
  harmful operating point. Matching WS across these two hierarchies is not
  possible, and the table must not be presented as a controlled comparison. It
  is two existence proofs, not an A/B.
- **MBA means different things on the two hosts; both were calibrated before
  use.** Intel (`mos182_mba_calib.jsonl`): percentage, `bandwidth_gran 10`,
  `delay_linear 1`, a *per-core* request delay. Settings 100 down to 30 all
  deliver 23.8-24.0 GB/s -- nothing at all -- and only 20 (22.57) and 10 (12.57)
  bind. The host offers exactly one usable throttle point below 95%. AMD
  (`moscxl_mba_calib.jsonl`): group-level cap in 1/8 GB/s units, 2048 steps;
  256 and above deliver 24.6 unthrottled, 128 delivers 15.36.
- **So the Intel MBA cell is not a weak-throttle artifact.** `MBA10` is the
  hardest setting the host has and it nearly halves the streamer. GPROBE states
  the recovery as 9% of the 1.621x (i.e. of the tax value); expressed as a
  fraction of the excess over 1.0 it is 24%. Either way it costs 47% of the
  streamer's bandwidth to get it, and once CAT is applied it buys nothing.
  **Whichever convention the paper uses, it must use one.**

## What this table does not establish

- **It does not restore L5 in any form.** CAT is a complete answer on Intel;
  CAT+MBA is a complete answer on AMD at 4% of the streamer's bandwidth. L5 is
  dead and this document does not revive it.
- **It does not identify the AMD mechanism as back-invalidation, and the
  temptation to read it that way must be resisted.** GPROBE S5.4 tested exactly
  that and rejected it: at WS=1024 the victim's L2 miss rate does move under
  co-run (26.4% -> 45.2%), but the `CAT1_nostream` control moves it too
  (26.2% -> 35.4%) *with no co-runner at all*, and an L3 way mask cannot
  invalidate a private L2. At WS=256, where the victim is unambiguously
  L2-resident, the miss rate does not move in any arm against a streamer
  holding the whole L3. The AMD harm is bandwidth and queueing.
  **Consequently this table does not close the gap between the two
  instruments.** gem5's benefit removes a back-invalidation charge; silicon
  here levies a capacity charge on Intel and a queueing charge on AMD. Neither
  is the charge H3 removes. W5.3 must not be written in a way that implies
  otherwise.
- **It does not show the AMD saturation window is universal.** One probe, one
  streamer, one WS, one machine. "The operating point must be found
  empirically" is supported; "the window is always ~4%" is not.
- **Nothing here measures Streaming.** It measures what the deployed
  alternatives do, which is the premise the contribution has to clear.

## What it does establish, and what contribution (2) should say

W5.2 proposes restating contribution (2) as "no deployed control can be *aimed
at the object*." This table is what makes that an earned position rather than a
retreat from a falsified claim:

- **CAT is aimed at a way mask.** On Intel that happens to be the right target
  and CAT wins outright. On AMD it is the wrong target and two thirds of the
  harm survives a three-quarter grant.
- **MBA is aimed at a thread's total request rate.** It charges the whole
  thread for one object's behaviour -- on Intel, 47% of the streamer's
  bandwidth for at most a quarter of the tax. It works on AMD only because the
  streamer there is doing nothing *but* streaming, and only within a few
  percent of a saturation point that has to be found per workload pair.
- **Neither is aimed at the allocation decision for a set of pages**, which is
  the thing that actually needs deciding, and which is the only granularity at
  which a control is both sufficient and free.

GPROBE S5.7 already draws the sharpest form of this and it should survive into
the paper verbatim in substance: a capacity-sensitivity admission gate
**mis-describes** the AMD harm and is **redundant** against the Intel harm. It
is wrong in one direction on one vendor and unnecessary in the other direction
on the other. That is a finding about the design, not just about the baselines,
and it is the reason S5.3 belongs early rather than in a limitations paragraph.

### One S1 correction this analysis forces

GPROBE S5.7's incidental finding: mos182 exposes **L2 CAT** --
`/sys/fs/resctrl/info/L2/` is populated, `cbm_mask ffff` (16 ways),
`num_closids 8`, one domain per core, with a live `L2:` line in the schemata.
S1's premise sentence "CAT cannot defend a private cache" is therefore false as
written on this silicon. The argument survives -- an invalidation is not an
allocation, and no L2 way mask prevents back-invalidation -- but the sentence
does not, and it is on page 1. Whether the interface actually enforces was not
tested. Added to the held paper-edit queue.
