# W4.6 — `tab:sens`'s associativity axis is a capacity axis, and the table is one curve, not two

Written 2026-08-24, after `W7.2_A1_SIZING_2026-08-24.md`. **Zero compute cost**
and, unusually, **zero dependence on surviving run artifacts**: the finding
follows deterministically from two committed launcher parameters and one line of
gem5 source. The `tab:sens` runs themselves are not needed and were not consulted
for the geometry.

## The mechanism, restated

`CacheMemory::init()` (`DutyFree-Gem5/src/mem/ruby/structures/CacheMemory.cc`)
computes `m_cache_num_sets = (size/assoc)/block` but indexes with
`m_cache_num_set_bits = floorLog2(m_cache_num_sets)`. Sets beyond
`2^floorLog2(num_sets)` are allocated and never addressed, so the **effective**
capacity is `2^floorLog2(num_sets) x assoc x block`. W7.2 found this on W7's A1
point. It also applies to a published table.

## The application to `tab:sens`

`experiments/asplos/p1batch.sh` launches the associativity sweep as

```
for A in 8 12 20; do
  launch a4_a$A "L3_ASSOC=$A" a4_a$A alone 0 0
  ...
```

and `experiments/asplos/b4run2.sh` turns that into
`--l3_size=5MiB --l3_assoc=$L3A`, with `WSS=${WSS:-2650}` — so the victim
footprint and the *configured* LLC size are both held while only associativity
moves. Both launchers are committed; `W4.3_PROVENANCE_LEDGER_2026-08-23.md`
already records this table as **VERIFIED**, apparatus intact. That is what makes
this finding checkable without the runs.

The realized capacity is not held:

| row | assoc | `num_sets` | `floorLog2` | sets indexed | **effective LLC** | fraction of 5 MiB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 8-way | 10,240 | 13 | 8,192 | **4 MiB** | 80% |
| 2 | 12-way | 6,826 | 12 | 4,096 | **3 MiB** | 60% |
| 3 | 20-way | 4,096 | 12 | 4,096 | **5 MiB — exact** | 100% |

The three rows ran at **5, 4 and 3 MiB**. The WSS sweep is unaffected: it holds
`L3_ASSOC` at its default 20 and varies only the victim's footprint, so all three
of its cells ran at an exact 5 MiB.

**The caption's "53% WSS" is wrong for two of three rows.** With the victim at
2650 KiB, WSS/LLC is 51.8% at 20-way, **64.7%** at 8-way and **86.3%** at 12-way.
(The published 53% comes from dividing by 5000 KiB rather than 5120; that is a
separate, trivial slip and it is not what this memo is about.)

## The table is one curve

Sorting all five non-trivial cells by WSS over *effective* LLC:

| WSS / effective LLC | cell | configured as | WB tax | H2 tax | recovered |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 24.4% | WSS 1250, 20-way | "24%, L2-resident" | 1.000 | 1.000 | — |
| 51.8% | WSS 2650, 20-way | "53%" **and** "20-way" | 1.369 | 1.041 | 88.8% |
| 64.7% | WSS 2650, 8-way | "8-way" | 1.478 | 1.044 | 90.8% |
| 86.3% | WSS 2650, 12-way | "12-way" | 1.586 | 1.047 | 91.9% |
| 97.7% | WSS 5000, 20-way | "97%" | 1.941 | 1.089 | 90.5% |

**Both arms are monotonic in WSS/effective-LLC, over all five cells, across both
sub-sweeps.** The published table's most distinctive feature — that the
associativity axis is non-monotonic, with 12-way worse than both 8-way and
20-way — disappears: 12-way is not an associativity outlier, it is simply the
smallest cache in the table.

`GATE1_SENS_RERUN_OUTCOME.md` treats that non-monotonicity as evidence of a
genuine reproduction: *"an unusual pattern that reproducing by coincidence would
be a bigger surprise than reproducing it for real."* The reproduction conclusion
stands — 12/12 cells, matched baselines, the exact L2-resident row — but this
particular argument is weaker than it reads. The shape is a deterministic
consequence of the flags, so any run at those flags produces it. It is explained
now, not merely reproduced.

## What may and may not be claimed

**Strengthened.** The recovery claim gets *wider*, not narrower. "88.8–91.9%
across LLC associativity and victim footprint" becomes **88.8–91.9% across an
effective LLC from 3 to 5 MiB, a victim footprint from 1250 to 5000 KiB, an
associativity from 8 to 20 ways, and a WSS/LLC ratio from 52% to 98%** — four
things varying at once and the recovery flat through all of them. That is a
better robustness result than the one in the paper.

**Weakened.** The claim that the recovery holds *across associativity* is
**confounded, not false**. Associativity really did vary 8/12/20; capacity varied
with it, uninstructed. This table cannot separate the two, and no sentence
should imply it can.

**Not determined.** Whether there is an associativity effect at all. Against a
straight line through the two 20-way WSS anchors (51.8% -> 1.369, 97.7% ->
1.941), the three assoc points fall 3–12% *below* the interpolation. With single
runs per cell, two anchors, and a linear guess doing the work, that residual is
not resolvable and is not evidence of anything. It is recorded so that nobody
later reads the monotonicity as proof that associativity does nothing.

## The clean re-run, if it is wanted

At a 5 MiB configured LLC the associativities whose set counts are exact are
**{5, 10, 20, 40, 80}** — `5242880/(assoc x 64)` must be a power of two, which
20 satisfies and 8, 12 and 16 all fail (16-way also lands on 4096 sets, i.e.
4 MiB). A sweep over **{5, 10, 20, 40}** varies associativity 8x at a genuinely
fixed 5 MiB and would settle the question. 20-way already exists, so it is 9 new
cells in the same 3-arm shape (`alone`/`wb`/`st`) `p1batch.sh` already launches,
at the §5.3 `ITERS=3e6` gate. The apparatus needs no change: `L3_ASSOC` is
already an env override on `b4run2.sh`.

Whether to spend it is not an analysis decision, and the paper does not require
it — the honest version of the existing table is stronger than the claimed one.

## Addendum, 2026-08-24 — the sweep is complete: three affected cells, and no others

Having found the defect twice, the obvious question is how many more times it
occurs. Every cache geometry reachable from a committed launcher in this
repository was enumerated and checked. Zero compute cost.

| structure | geometry | `num_sets` | power of two? | effective |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| L1d | 48 KiB / 12-way | 64 | yes | 48 KiB |
| L1i | 32 KiB / 8-way | 64 | yes | 32 KiB |
| L2 | 2 MiB / 16-way | 2,048 | yes | 2 MiB |
| L2 | 512 KiB / 16-way (W7 A1) | 512 | yes | 512 KiB |
| L2 | 512 KiB / 8-way (`intel_latency_test.sh`) | 1,024 | yes | 512 KiB |
| LLC | 5 MiB / 20-way | 4,096 | yes | 5 MiB |
| LLC | 2 MiB / 16-way (`intel_8462y_*`) | 2,048 | yes | 2 MiB |
| LLC | 16 MiB / 16-way (`intel_latency_test.sh`) | 16,384 | yes | 16 MiB |
| **LLC** | **5 MiB / 8-way** (`tab:sens` row 1) | 10,240 | **no** | **4 MiB** |
| **LLC** | **5 MiB / 12-way** (`tab:sens` row 2) | 6,826 | **no** | **3 MiB** |
| **LLC** | **32 MiB / 20-way** (W7 A1) | 26,214 | **no** | **20 MiB** |
| snoop filter | 4,096 sets x 16 ways | 4,096 | yes | 65,536 entries |
| snoop filter | 512 sets x 8 ways (`b4run*.sh` default) | 512 | yes | 4,096 entries |

Sources swept: `experiments/asplos/{b4run.sh,b4run2.sh,p0batch.sh,p1batch.sh,
sf_inf_cells.sh,sf_fin_cells.sh,sfsweep.sh,w7_campaign.sh}` and
`DutyFree-Gem5/scripts/{run_se.sh,intel_8592_4cpu_dirtax_streaming*.sh,
intel_8592_8cpu_dirtax_streaming.sh,intel_8462y_*cpu_dirtax_streaming.sh,
intel_latency_test.sh,fs_restore_chi_8592.sh}`.

**Three affected cells, all LLC, all already documented** — two by this memo and
one by `W7.2_A1_SIZING_2026-08-24.md`. Everything else in the repository is
exact, including every L1, every L2, and both snoop-filter geometries.

Two structural reasons the blast radius is small, worth recording because they
are what makes the "no others" claim checkable rather than merely surveyed:

1. **The snoop filter cannot be affected by construction.** `CHI_config_8592.py`
   sizes it as `size = sf_sets * sf_ways * 64` with `assoc = sf_ways`, so
   `num_sets` is identically `sf_sets` — the set count is set directly rather
   than derived from a byte size, and every value used is a power of two.
2. **The LLC is the only structure whose size and associativity are both swept.**
   L1 and L2 geometries are fixed across every campaign; `--l3_size` is swept by
   `w7_campaign.sh` and `--l3_assoc` by `p1batch.sh`, and each sweep produced
   exactly one bad cell that the other could not have caught.

**Also unaffected: `--num-l3caches`.** Slice count changes `start_index_bit` via
`intlvHighBit`, i.e. *which* address bits index the set, not how many sets are
reachable. The multi-slice W8 arms (`fs_restore_chi_8592.sh`, 5 MiB per slice,
20-way) are exact.

**Not swept, and out of scope:** the classic (non-Ruby) cache hierarchy used by
W8's `--caches` atomic boot, which is a different cache model with different
indexing, and gem5 upstream's own example configs, which no result of ours
depends on.
