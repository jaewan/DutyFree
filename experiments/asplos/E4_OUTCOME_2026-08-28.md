- **P1 (the wedge collapses) does NOT fire.** It required <= 8% at 100%

- **P3 (knee near 100%) HOLDS**: +2.0% at 67% occupancy, +42.9% at 200%.

> **Across a 5.3x cache-size change and a 4x way-size change, confining a fused
> tenant costs the same function of how full its partition is** --- nothing below
> two thirds, ~15--17% at capacity, 40--57% beyond. Matched to 0.1 points at 57%

This is the paper's case, and it is now a property of context-scoped partitioning
rather than of one machine.

# The reconciliation E4 forces

E1's frontier claimed the confinement cost is **a function of partition occupancy,
not of way count**, and supported it by collapsing 15 cells onto one curve. E3
then found that curve's knee moves between hosts (~2/3 on mos181, ~100% on
mos182), and I recorded that as "the coefficient is platform-specific."

**That framing was wrong, and E4 shows why.** Put both parties' curves side by
side:

### Tenant --- 16 threads, hash-join probe, morsel-driven
| occupancy | 57% | 67% | 100% | 200% | 400% |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| mos181 | +0.3% | +2.1% | +16.9% | +41.5% | +43.2% |
| mos182 | +0.2% | +2.0% | +15.0% | +42.9% | +56.9% |

### Victim --- 1 thread, random pointer chase
| occupancy | 60% | 80% | 89% | 120% | 160% | 250% |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| mos181 | +0.1% | **+10.8%** | **+13.1%** | +65.2% | +84.4% | +137.7% |
| mos182 | +0.0% | **+0.2%** | **+0.3%** | +57.1% | +118.8% | +181.5% |

**The victim's curves agree below 60% (both zero) and above 120% (both large).
They diverge in exactly one band: 75--90%, where mos181 pays 10.8--13.1% and
mos182 pays 0.2--0.3%.**

So the correct statement is not "the coefficient is platform-specific." It is:

> **There are two curves, belonging to two access patterns, and E1 conflated them
> because pass A measured only the tenant and pass B measured only the victim ---
> and on mos181 the two happened to have similar knees.**

- The **tenant's** curve --- 16 threads, independent accesses, spatial locality
  within a morsel --- is governed by capacity and is **host-invariant**. Capacity
  is capacity.
- The **victim's** curve --- one thread, dependent random loads, no
  prefetchability --- has an **additional near-capacity onset** that mos181
  exhibits and mos182 does not. Below 60% there is nothing to conflict over; above
  120% the set is simply too big for the partition on any part. In between, whether
  you pay depends on conflict behaviour, and that is where the two parts differ.

## What survives from the frontier, precisely

| E1's claim | status |
|---|---|
| cost is a function of occupancy, not way count | **survives within a host** --- E3a's matched-occupancy cells agree to 2.1 points across a 2.8x range of way counts |
| the same function on any host | **false for the victim**, in the 75--90% band |
| the same function on any host | **true for the tenant**, across both geometries |
| no static split satisfies both parties | **survives on both hosts** (below) |
| "a free split exists iff combined WS <= 2/3 x LLC" | **withdrawn as a law** --- the coefficient is per-party and per-host |

The no-free-split conclusion is unaffected because it is a sum of two
independently measured requirements, not a single coefficient:

| host | tenant needs | victim needs | total | LLC | free split? |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--|
| mos181 | 192 MiB (1.5x its 128 MiB table) | 259 MiB (1.6x its 162 MiB set) | **451 MiB** | 320 MiB | **no** |
| mos182 | 48 MiB (1.5x its 32 MiB table) | 55 MiB (1.15x its 48 MiB set) | **103 MiB** | 60 MiB | **no** |

Note the tenant's multiplier is **1.5x on both hosts**; the victim's is 1.6x on
one and 1.15x on the other. That is the same asymmetry, expressed as a
requirement rather than a curve.

## A candidate mechanism, offered as a hypothesis

The divergence is confined to a random-access workload near capacity, and the two
parts differ structurally in exactly the way that would matter there:

| | sets per way-set | CHA slices | MiB per way |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| mos181 | **262,144** | ~64 | 16 |
| mos182 | **65,536** | ~32 | 4 |

A random walk's lines distribute across sets and slices by address hash. Near
capacity, whether a given set overflows its ways depends on that distribution, and
mos181 has 4x the sets spread over 2x the slices. A workload with spatial
locality (the tenant) fills sets more evenly and is less exposed to the tail of
that distribution.

**This is not demonstrated.** Simple Poisson estimates of per-set overflow
probability come out similar for the two hosts' 86--89% cells (~0.21 vs ~0.23), so
the naive statistical account does *not* explain a 13.1%-versus-0.3% gap, and
something more specific --- slice hashing, replacement policy detail, or an
EMR/SPR difference we have not identified --- is doing the work. Separating them
needs a footprint-matched sweep across hosts, which is cheap and is not run.

## Consequence for the paper

**State the trade-off on the tenant's axis.** That axis is measured on two
geometries, agrees to 1.9 points at the decisive cell, and is the axis the case
actually rests on: partitioning charges a fused tenant ~15--17% at capacity and
40%+ beyond, and an object label removes the stream's demand without incurring
that charge.

**Demote the neighbour's counter-charge to a platform-conditional observation.**
13.1% on mos181 is real and worth reporting, but it is 0.2% on mos182 in the same
occupancy band, so it cannot carry weight in the headline claim. Anything written
on it must name the host.

That is a narrower headline than `M12_OUTCOME`'s two-sided table implied, and a
sturdier one: it moves the claim off the number that does not travel and onto the
number that does.

## What this cannot show

Two Intel geometries, both way-partitioned, both 2 MiB private L2. One tenant
workload and one victim workload, so "access pattern governs which curve you get"
is an inference from two points, not a characterisation. AMD is untested and its
harm is rate-class rather than capacity-class. And the tenant's cost remains the
cost of *confinement*; what a real memory type would cost the tenant is still
unmeasured on silicon (M12 pass A).
