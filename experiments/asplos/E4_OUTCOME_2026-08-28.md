# E4 outcome: the wedge travels. The tenant's cost is a property of the mechanism, not of one machine.

Pre-registration `E4_WEDGE_PREREG_2026-08-28.md` (`da5a6cd`). mos182 (SPR 8462Y+,
60 MiB / **15 ways x 4 MiB**), tenant table 32 MiB, paired ratios, **n=12, 60/60
pairs**, `cpus_list` asserted `16-31` on every run, mask bit-counted, preflight
passed. Instrument check **PASSES**: unmasked baseline CoV **0.16%** across all 60
pairs against a registered <= 2%.

## Matched on occupancy, the two machines agree

| occupancy | partition (mos182) | **mos182 cost** | **mos181 cost** | difference |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 400% | 8 MiB (2 ways) | +56.9% | +43.2% | 13.7 pts |
| 200% | 16 MiB (4 ways) | +42.9% | +41.5% | 1.4 pts |
| **100%** | **32 MiB (8 ways)** | **+15.0%** | **+16.9%** | **1.9 pts** |
| **67%** | **48 MiB (12 ways)** | **+2.0%** | **+2.1%** | **0.1 pts** |
| 57% / 40% | 56 MiB (14 ways) | +0.2% | +0.3% | 0.1 pts |

Ratio CoV **0.20--1.14%** at every width --- no bimodality anywhere, unlike
mos181's masked arms, so the medians are solid and the guard did not fire.

- **P2 FIRES**: at 100% occupancy the cost is 15.0% against mos181's 16.9%, a
  **1.9-point** difference against an 8-point threshold.
- **P1 does not fire**: the wedge does not collapse.
- **P3 HOLDS**: <= 3% at 67% occupancy (+2.0%) and >= 8% at 200% (+42.9%), so the
  tenant's knee sits between 67% and 100% --- **the same place it sits on mos181.**

**The paper's central number survives a 5.3x change in cache size and a 4x change
in way size.** This was the cheapest available test that could have invalidated the
case, and it passed.

## But the registered "incoherent" branch also fired, and it is the real finding

E4's registration named this outcome in advance: *"P3 fires with P2 --- incoherent,
and the most interesting outcome: the victim's knee moves between hosts but the
tenant's does not. That would mean the two costs have different mechanisms, and
the frontier's single-curve account is wrong."*

That is exactly what happened:

| party | knee on mos181 | knee on mos182 | travels? |
|---|---|---|:--|
| **tenant** (16 threads, streaming + hash probe) | 67--100% | **67--100%** | **yes** |
| **victim** (1 thread, random pointer chase) | 66--75% | **86--120%** | **no** |

So E1's single-curve account --- "confinement cost is a function of partition
occupancy" --- is **too simple**. It holds within a host and within a party, but the
*location* of the knee is party-dependent, and it moves for one party and not the
other across the same pair of machines.

### Leading hypothesis, offered as such

The confinement charge has **two** components, and their weight depends on the
access pattern:

- **capacity** --- how full the partition is. Governs the tenant, whose streaming
  probe is MLP-rich and can absorb individual misses.
- **associativity** --- how many ways the partition has. Governs the victim, whose
  dependent random walk stalls fully on every conflict miss with no MLP to hide it.

That predicts what we see: the victim held 12 of 20 ways on mos181 but **14 of 15**
on mos182 --- *more* associativity at similar occupancy --- so its conflict misses
fall and its knee moves later. The tenant, being capacity-bound, tracks occupancy
regardless. It also explains E3's otherwise odd pass-A result that way count
mattered for the victim **above** the knee (5 ways +79% vs 10 ways +57% at the same
120%) but not below.

**This is a hypothesis from two hosts and two workloads.** Testing it needs way
count and occupancy varied independently at fixed capacity --- e.g. the victim at
matched occupancy on 5 vs 14 ways --- which E3's pass A partly did and which a
dedicated sweep would settle.

## What this does to the paper's claim structure

The trade-off has two halves and they are **not** equally solid:

| half | status |
|---|---|
| **tenant side** --- CAT charges a fused tenant 15--43% because it cannot separate the tenant's stream from the tenant's own reuse | **stable across geometries.** Load-bearing. |
| **victim side** --- CAT charges the protected neighbour 0.2--13.1% | **platform-dependent.** Secondary. |

So the argument should be **built on the tenant side**:

> Way-partitioning protects a neighbour completely --- interference goes to zero at
> every split we measured. But it charges the *streaming tenant* 15% at parity and
> up to 57% when squeezed, because a context-scoped mask cannot separate that
> thread's one-pass stream from its own reused structure. That charge reproduces
> across a 5.3x cache-size and 4x way-size change, so it is a property of
> context-scoped partitioning rather than of one part. An object-scoped label
> removes the stream's demand without confining anyone.

The neighbour's own confinement cost becomes a **conditional bonus**, stated with
its platform dependence, rather than a leg the argument stands on. That is a
narrower claim than `M12_OUTCOME` implied and a **better-supported** one.

## What is still missing, unchanged by E4

The label's side of that comparison remains unmeasured. The silicon proxy costs the
tenant 13--19% --- more than the 15% it would save --- and gem5 has neither
way-partitioning nor a workload in the regime where admission acts. E4 makes those
two jobs **better motivated**, not less necessary: we now know the number the label
has to beat, and that it is stable.

## What this cannot show

mos182 only for this pass, one table size, one hit rate, one stream size, no
victim. Both hosts Intel and way-partitioned. The 400% cell differs by 13.7 points
between hosts and is not explained; the paper's operating range is 67--100%, where
they agree to within 1.9 points.
