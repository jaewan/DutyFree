# W1.2 — the H3 arm is not the H3 the paper describes

> **Revised 2026-08-23 23:41, before any H3/infinite data exists** (the
> `sf_h3_inf_*` runs have not started). Two claims below are wrong and one
> prediction is superseded. The original text is left intact — see
> §"Revision" at the end. Read that section before acting on this one.

Written 2026-08-23, while sourcing W6.1's "the mechanism is nearly free" claim
to the actual gem5 diff. The H3/infinite-SF runs have **not started** (only
`sf_h2_inf_s{1,2,3}` are in flight), so §5's prediction is pre-registered
against data that does not yet exist.

## What the paper says H3 is

Do not enrol STREAMING lines in the HNF snoop filter, so the streamer's
footprint cannot evict the victim's SF entries and back-invalidate its private
caches.

## What the implemented H3 does

Two things, not one. From `~/DutyFree-Gem5`:

1. **The SF skip.** `44b7eb7470` guards three HNF directory operations on
   `is_HN && tbe.isStreaming`: `directory.allocate` is skipped, the
   final-state directory update is skipped, and the dir state machine is
   short-circuited (`dir_state` stays `I`). This is the paper's H3.

2. **No private-cache retention, anywhere.** `0102eee441` widened
   `needCacheEntry`'s streaming disjunct from `(is_HN && is_streaming)` to
   `((is_HN || enable_H3_streaming_bypass) && is_streaming)`, and
   `CHI_config_8592.py` sets `enable_H3_streaming_bypass = HNF_H3` on the
   **L1 controller (:284) and the L2 controller (:332)** as well as the HNF.
   Under `HNF_H3=1` a streaming line is therefore retained in *no* cache —
   not L3, not L2, not L1. The reads are issued as `ReadOnce`.

(2) is not a design choice recorded anywhere in the paper or in
`H3SF_REMEASURED_2026-08-20.md`. Its commit message states its purpose: with
no sharer recorded at the HNF, a later clean Evict/WB from L1/L2 arrives for
an untracked line and trips the `in_msg.stale == is_stale` assert. It was
added to make the arm run.

## The effect is large and it is on the aggressor

From the surviving finite-SF seed-1 stats (`/tmp/sf_{wb,h2,h3}_fin_s1`).
cpu0 is the victim (`dutyfree/victim 2650 3000000`), cpu1 the streamer
(`dutyfree/aggressor 16.0 stream`):

| arm | victim tax | streamer IPC | streamer L1D miss | streamer L2 miss | victim L2 miss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WB / finite SF | 2.501x | 0.1628 | 0.9534 | 0.4447 | 0.3147 |
| H2 / finite SF | 2.512x | 0.1853 | 0.7902 | 0.4490 | 0.3163 |
| H2+H3 / finite SF | 1.061x | **0.1131** | **0.9974** | **0.9063** | **0.2422** |

Both channels are visible and they are entangled:

- **The intended channel.** The victim's own L2 miss rate falls 0.3163 ->
  0.2422 (-23%). That is back-invalidation relief; nothing else in the H3 diff
  can move the victim's private cache.
- **The undisclosed channel.** The streamer's L2 stops working — miss rate
  0.449 -> 0.906, L1D 0.790 -> 0.997, i.e. essentially every access misses
  everywhere. Its IPC falls 39% relative to the H2 arm. A slower aggressor
  contends less, which helps the victim for a reason that is not H3.

So `tab:h3sf`'s 1.061x is an **upper bound** on the SF-skip's benefit, and the
attribution of the whole 2.512x -> 1.061x swing to snoop-filter enrolment is
unsafe as written.

## How much of it is the confound? A bound, not a de-confound

The WB -> H2 pair is the only in-hand lever on aggressor rate at fixed SF
geometry: it speeds the streamer up 13.8% (0.1628 -> 0.1853) and costs the
victim 0.4% more tax (2.501 -> 2.512). Sensitivity ~0.032 tax-fraction per
unit aggressor-IPC-fraction. Applied to H3's -39% streamer IPC, the rate
channel predicts about **-1.25% victim tax**, against an observed -57.7%.

On that estimate the confound explains roughly 2% of the effect and H3's
benefit survives. The estimate is **not** a controlled de-confound: WB -> H2
changes aggressor rate by removing L3 fills, which also changes HNF occupancy,
so the lever is not rate-only. W2.1 (P0-1 bandwidth-matched) remains required
and now has a concrete target: it must vary streamer rate *without* touching
retention.

## Is the confound necessary, or an artifact?

Artifact. The `stale` assert that motivated (2) fires because L1/L2 hold a line
the HNF is not tracking. A silicon design that skips SF enrolment must handle
exactly that case — a clean eviction of an untracked line is dropped, not
flagged. The separable arm (SF skip, private caches still retain) is
implementable; it is what the pre-`0102eee441` code was trying to be.

**This is the single most valuable follow-up run in the plan**, because it
converts an upper bound into an attribution. It is one config flag: leave
`enable_H3_streaming_bypass` set on the HNF and clear it on the L1/L2
controllers, and handle the untracked clean evict.

## Pre-registered prediction, fixed before the H3/infinite runs exist (Sec6.6)

`PLAN_B_REBUILD.md`'s W1 consistency check says H2+H3/infinite "should be
indistinguishable from H2/infinite — with an infinite SF there is no enrolment
charge for H3 to remove. If they differ, H3 is doing something unmodelled."

Given (2), that check is now **expected to fail**, and its failure is no longer
diagnostic of the SF story:

- **Prediction:** H2+H3/infinite will be measurably *better* than
  H2/infinite, because the no-retention change is SF-independent and slows the
  streamer in both geometries.
- **Predicted magnitude:** small. By the sensitivity above, a 39% streamer
  slowdown buys ~1-2% victim tax. So H2+H3/inf should land within a few
  percent of H2/inf, not near the finite-SF 2.4x swing.
- **If instead H2+H3/inf is much better than H2/inf** (say >10%), the
  no-retention channel is far stronger than the WB->H2 lever suggests, the 2%
  bound above is wrong, and `tab:h3sf`'s attribution must be withdrawn pending
  the separable-arm run.
- The WB/infinite comparator (1.369x) is unaffected by either channel.

Recorded before the runs. Whatever they show, the interpretation above is not
negotiable after the fact.

## Consequence for W6

W6.1's claim survives and gets sharper, because it is now sourced. Across
`5bdfcd8e19` (H2), `44b7eb7470` + `0102eee441` (H3) and `b49615b3c7`
(prefetch propagation), the mechanism adds:

- **zero new coherence states**, zero new events, zero new CHI request types
  (grep of every `+` line in the protocol diffs finds no added `State:` or
  `CHIRequestType:` enumerator; H3's `State:` references are reads in an
  existing predicate);
- **one bit** carried on `CHIRequestMsg`, the TBE, and the private
  `CacheEntry` (1 bit per 512 bits of data = 0.20% of a private tag+data
  array);
- **two predicate edits** on the fill path — one disjunct in `needCacheEntry`,
  one conjunct in `CheckCacheFill`'s `need_fill` — plus, for H3, three guards
  on HNF directory operations;
- on the x86 side, one PTE flag, one `TlbEntry::streaming` field, and one
  `Request::STREAMING_BIT` riding the **existing** `cacheCoherenceFlags` path
  that already carries GLC/SLC. No new datapath.

Two honesty items W6 must carry:

- **The gem5 implementation does not use PAT slot 6.** It uses a bespoke
  `EmulationPageTable::Streaming` flag in SE mode. The PAT encoding is
  asserted, not demonstrated; W8 (FS mode) is what would close it.
- **`5bdfcd8e19` also removed `is_prefetch ||` from `needCacheEntry`**
  ("Intel NINE accurate"), which is a *baseline* change bundled into the
  mechanism commit. Within-campaign arm comparisons are unaffected because
  both arms share the binary, but "STREAMING vs. stock gem5" is not what any
  of our numbers measure.
- **H3's correctness rests on the OS contract, not on hardware.** Skipping SF
  enrolment is only safe because I0/I1 guarantee no writer exists. That is the
  paper's best cost argument and it is currently unstated: the hardware is
  cheap *because* the OS pays.

# Revision, 2026-08-23 23:41

Written after reading `H3_MODELCHECK.md` and `Sec5_Evaluation.tex:243-250`,
and after pulling the streamer's *traffic* rates rather than only its
instruction rate. The `sf_h3_inf_*` runs still do not exist, so the
superseded prediction below is replaced before any data could have informed
it.

## Correction 1 — "disclosed nowhere" is false

The no-retention behaviour **is** on record, in two places I had not read:

- `H3_MODELCHECK.md` §(a) names it exactly — *"ReadOnce / no-retention — what
  we actually model and measure (tab:h3sf's H3 arm). A streaming read is never
  retained past its own use"* — and carries a measured streamer-side cost from
  a different (b4b_*, 2026-08-03) configuration: **WB 4.34 / H2 5.12 / H3
  1.22 GB/s**, attributed to lost prefetch MLP.
- `Sec5_Evaluation.tex:245-250`, a `\jw{}` comment, lists as an honest gap
  *"bandwidth-matched H2-vs-H3 control … to de-confound the recovery magnitude
  from the ReadOnce bandwidth loss"*, and the body at :243-244 says *"A
  retain-but-unenrolled realization remains open."*

What is true, and is the residue of the finding: the confound is **not in the
body text as a qualifier on the number**, and `H3SF_REMEASURED_2026-08-20.md`
— the document that produced the 1.061x now in `tab:h3sf` — does not carry it.
The gap is between what the project knows and what the table's reader is told.

## Correction 2 — the separable arm is not "what the pre-fix code was trying to be"

I wrote that the naive separable arm (skip SF enrolment, keep L1/L2 retention)
is implementable and that `0102eee441` was a simulator artifact working around
it. That is wrong, and the counterexample already exists.
`H3_MODELCHECK.md` §(d) is exactly that design — *"Retain-but-unenrolled —
UNSOUND, and now TLC-confirmed"*: `EndEpoch` reopens the line with no
reconciliation, and TLC's counterexample (state 5, `variantD_unsound`) shows a
retained copy the directory never heard about.

The separable arm is still buildable, but it is **(b) epoch-tagged retention
with a bulk clear at exit** or **(c) DeNovo-style self-invalidation** — both
TLC-clean, both retaining (so the streamer keeps its MLP), and both paying a
drain at the epoch boundary. So the follow-up run I called "one config flag" is
not one config flag; it is variant (b) or (c), and its cost is the drain, which
is the same unmeasured quantity W6.2 flags on the OS side.

## Correction 3 — the confound runs the *wrong way*, and this strengthens tab:h3sf

I inferred "slower aggressor contends less" from a 39% IPC drop. Traffic rates
say otherwise. Per streamer cycle, from the same seed-1 stats:

| arm | streamer IPC | streamer L1D miss/cyc | **streamer L2 miss/cyc** | HNF demand acc/cyc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WB / finite SF | 0.1628 | 0.2070 | 0.01151 | — |
| H2 / finite SF | 0.1853 | 0.1952 | 0.01097 | 0.01462 |
| H2+H3 / finite SF | 0.1131 | 0.1504 | **0.01728** | 0.02390 |

Under H3 the streamer executes 39% fewer instructions per cycle but pushes
**58% more traffic per cycle out of its private caches** (0.01097 -> 0.01728
L2 misses/cycle), because the L2 no longer filters anything. HNF demand
accesses per cycle rise 63% (this counter aggregates both cores, so read it as
corroborating, not as the streamer's alone).

So the shared level sees *more* streamer pressure per unit time under H3, not
less. The channel I flagged as inflating H3's benefit does not operate in this
apparatus; if anything it works against the measured recovery. The victim's L2
miss rate nevertheless falls 0.3163 -> 0.2422 while the streamer loads the HNF
harder — which is what SF-enrolment relief should look like and is hard to get
any other way.

**Net effect on the paper:** `tab:h3sf`'s attribution is in better shape than
§"The effect is large" claims, not worse. The 1.061x should still be described
as measured under a no-retention realization rather than the retain-and-skip
mechanism the text implies, and `H3_MODELCHECK.md`'s taxonomy is the right
thing to cite for why that realization was chosen. Withdraw the "upper bound"
framing; replace it with "measured under realization (a), whose streamer-side
cost is known and whose sound retaining alternatives (b)/(c) are unbuilt."

The earlier ~2% arithmetic (a 0.032 tax-per-IPC sensitivity from the WB->H2
lever) is also withdrawn: it extrapolated a 13.8% lever across a much larger
change, and it assumed the wrong sign.

## Superseded prediction, and its replacement

**Superseded** (registered ~22:40 today): *H2+H3/infinite will be measurably
better than H2/infinite, by a few percent, because no-retention slows the
streamer in both geometries.* Its premise — that no-retention helps the victim
by throttling the streamer — is what Correction 3 refutes.

**Replacement, registered 2026-08-23 23:41, `sf_h3_inf_*` not started:**

- **Prediction:** H2+H3/infinite will be **the same as or slightly worse than**
  H2/infinite. With an infinite SF there is no enrolment charge to remove, so
  H3's only remaining effect is the no-retention change, which raises the
  streamer's traffic per cycle into the shared level and should cost the victim
  a little.
- **Predicted magnitude:** within a few percent either way; I will call
  anything inside ±5% "the same."
- **If H2+H3/inf is much better than H2/inf (>10%)**, H3 is removing a charge
  that survives an infinite SF, which no current account explains, and
  `tab:h3sf`'s H3 attribution must be withdrawn pending investigation.
- **If H2+H3/inf is much worse than H2/inf (>10%)**, the no-retention traffic
  penalty is large and the finite-SF H3 recovery is a *net* of a big penalty
  and a bigger relief — which would make variant (b)/(c) more valuable, not
  less, and should be said in the paper.
- The WB/infinite comparator (1.369x) is unaffected either way.

Both predictions stay on the record. Whichever way the runs land, the
interpretation above is fixed now.

## Correction 4 (same revision) — the paper discloses it in the Appendix

`Appendix.tex:422-424`: *"H3 additionally issues the read as `ReadOnce`, which
forfeits private retention and is what makes skipping snoop-filter enrollment
coherent."* And `:461-465`: *"Our H3 realization issues streaming reads as
`ReadOnce`, forfeiting private retention … A retain-but-unenrolled realization
would be sound only under strict immutability and is left open."*

Both are correct and both are consistent with `H3_MODELCHECK.md` §(d): the TLC
counterexample requires a write, so "unsound in general, sound under strict
immutability" is precise, not contradictory.

So the residue of W1.2 is smaller again, and it is now exactly one thing: the
disclosure lives in the Appendix while `tab:h3sf`'s number is used in Sec5
without it, which is the §5.1 arm-identity rule. Add the realization name where
the 1.061x is quoted, and carry it into
`H3SF_REMEASURED_2026-08-20.md`, which has it nowhere. Everything else in this
document's original §"What the implemented H3 does" was already known to the
project; what was new is Correction 3's traffic-direction measurement, which
stands.

## Resolution, 2026-08-24

The replacement prediction registered above at 23:41 on 2026-08-23 has been
evaluated against data that did not exist when it was written. Outcome:
`W1.5_H3_INFINITE_SF_OUTCOME_2026-08-24.md`.

(H2+H3)/infinite / H2/infinite = **1.0345** -- 3.45% worse, inside the +/-5%
band, in the predicted direction. No withdrawal triggered.

Correction 3 above -- that the traffic confound runs *against* `tab:h3sf`, not
for it -- is independently confirmed at an infinite SF: under H2+H3 the streamer
puts **+61.7%** more traffic per cycle onto the shared fabric than under H2
alone, against the +58% Correction 3 measured at a finite SF. Its L1D hit rate
is 0.3% and its private-L2 hit rate 11.7%, which is no-retention observed
directly rather than inferred.
