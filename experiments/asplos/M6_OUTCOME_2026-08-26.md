## R1's registered verdict, and it is worse than the branch anticipated

**CAT-narrow reaches the corner** — V ≤ 1.05× with F's cost inside 5% at 4 MiB.
The registered consequence was "division of labour, not sole rescue". But pass B
shows CAT-narrow reaching the corner in **all four** cells, including the
256 MiB table where the label does not. So the head-to-head is:

| | V protection | cost to F |
|---|--:|--:|
| **4 MiB — label (flush)** | 0.9928 | **+18.7%** |
| **4 MiB — CAT-narrow** | 0.9924 | **+2.9%** |
| **256 MiB — label (flush)** | **2.2776** | −3.3% |
| **256 MiB — CAT-narrow** | **0.9899** | +7.5% |

At 4 MiB the shipped knob **dominates the label on both axes**. At 256 MiB the
label does not protect V at all while the knob does, for +7.5%.

> **The label has no winning cell in this experiment.**

## R2, falsified in pass A before pass B ran

The registered prediction was that under a narrow mask, flushing would help F,
because removing the stream's churn from F's own mask should pay for the flush.
It does not: F goes 55.90 → 65.94 cyc/access, **+18.0% worse**. The composition
mechanism I proposed is absent — the proxy's intrinsic cost exceeds what it saves
inside the mask. Pass B's V-side confirms there is nothing to buy there either
(0.9899 retained vs 0.9906 flushed).

One asymmetry worth keeping: at 256 MiB with **no** CAT, flushing slightly
*helps* F (52.00 → 50.29, −3.3%). So the stream was genuinely evicting F's own
table, and non-allocation recovers a little — but only when F holds the whole
cache. Confine F and the flush cost dominates.

## What this does to the paper's central argument

The argument was that CAT cannot express the fused case: one thread, two access
classes, one label. **That is still true as stated — and M6 shows it does not
matter for protecting a neighbour.** You do not need to separate F's two access
classes to protect V; you confine F wholesale, on its own cores, and V keeps the
complement. The inexpressibility only bites if you care about F's *own*
performance while protecting V — and there the measured price of confining F
wholesale is +2.9% to +7.5%, not the +19–44% the paper reports.

**That +19–44% needs a caveat, and it is the same one that has bitten twice
already.** `tab:fused`'s monotone column was measured at **hit rate 0.5**, where
F's probe misses half the time and walks chains across the whole table, so any
mask starves it. At hit rate 1.0 the same class of restriction costs F **+7.5%**.
The exhibit is hit-rate specific and must say so.

## What survives

- The **two-component account** stands and is now sharper: stream residency,
  which non-allocation removes completely (M5's 0.993× cell reproduced here at
  0.9928); and the tenant's own working set, which nothing should remove.
- The **taxonomy** stands: no shipped mechanism is address-scoped *and* enforced.
- **RocksDB's demand evidence** stands, untouched by any of this.
- **W5.3's boundary map** stands and is reinforced: shipped knobs work, and now
  in one more configuration than we had measured.

## What does not

The claim that the mechanism is *needed*. On this platform, in this
configuration, a two-way CAT mask does everything the label does and more, for
less. The honest sentence is that the label removes a component partitioning
cannot name, that the component is real and completely removable, and that on
Intel EMR **partitioning the tenant's cores achieves the same neighbour
protection more cheaply** — so the label's case must rest on configurations
where core-scoped confinement is unavailable or unacceptable, and **we have not
measured one.**

## Caveats

- Intel EMR only; broker unreachable four days.
- The label is represented by a software proxy costing 14–19%; the type's
  zero-cost version is simulator-bounded, and gem5 cannot model the component
  that dominates silicon harm (`GEM5_TRANSPORT_CHECK_2026-08-26.md`). A zero-cost
  label would beat the proxy on F's axis — but it would still not beat CAT-narrow
  on V's axis at 256 MiB, where it leaves V at 2.28×.
- CPU-based CLOS works here because V and F are on different cores. A tenant that
  must share a core with its victim is not covered, and is the obvious place to
  look for a configuration the label wins — untested.
