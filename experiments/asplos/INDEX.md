## How to read a document

| suffix | meaning |
|---|---|
| `*_PREREG_*` | thresholds and action-on-miss, fixed **before** the data existed. **Read "committed" out of that sentence, which is what it used to say:** all fourteen campaign registrations are now in git, but for **ten** of them the registration was committed *after* their data (**this read `nine` until a birth-time re-audit on 2026-09-04**, quoted rather than deleted per `A6.19`; the recount is in `F16` and in the buckets below), so what the suffix records is the document's own dated attestation and not a commit order. Every row below now says which of the two it holds (`F16` in `A1_PROVENANCE_LEDGER_2026-08-28.md`) |
| `*_OUTCOME_*` | the result, judged against that pre-registration |
| `*_CORRECTION_*` / `*_RETRACTION_*` | a previously published number withdrawn |
| `*_CLOSED_*` | a campaign's **disposition**, recorded without new compute: where its verdict already lives, or why none is coming. Not a result and not a withdrawal — read it for the pointer, then read what it points at. Three instances: `W3.1_CLOSED_2026-08-23.md`, `FUSED_KNEE_CLOSED_2026-09-04.md`, `H2H_REALJOIN_CLOSED_2026-09-04.md` |
| addenda inside a file | later findings appended, never edited in place (rule A6.19) |
| **`AMENDED`** at the head of a row below | that document's **body still states something a later addendum withdrew**. The row names the withdrawn claim and the addendum that withdrew it |

`AMENDED` exists because of `A6.19`. Corrections are appended and never written
back into the body, so a reader who finds only the original section comes away
holding a withdrawn claim — stated confidently, and often in several sections at
once. The marker is not a quality judgement; appending is the correct practice
and these documents all followed it. It means one thing: **do not quote the body
of this document without reading its addenda.** When you append an addendum that
withdraws something, add the marker here in the same pass.

A document whose numbers were later invalidated carries a **`SUPERSEDED`** header
pointing at its replacement. Trust the pointer, not the filename date.

was taken from a row that turned out not to support it, and is restated here
rather than quietly fixed.** It read: "following the convention

launch**\")" — quoted per `A6.19`, and false in its second half. That

reported"**, and where a row can honestly claim precedence over *launch* it says

**Dates on those citations, recorded here because the mismatch keeps being
rediscovered.** This host's clock is **KST (UTC+9) and runs a day ahead** of the
project-local `UTC-7` by which every record in this directory is dated. Measured
2026-09-04: **54** of that day's commits carry author dates reading
`2026-09-05` while the records inside them read `2026-09-04`. So a freeze
citation whose `git show` prints `2026-09-05` is not evidence that the record
naming it is misdated. **Rewriting those author dates is foreclosed, not merely
inadvisable**: 22 of the 32 commit hashes this file and
`A1_PROVENANCE_LEDGER_2026-08-28.md` cite by name are among the 54, so a rewrite
would change the hashes the freeze citations are made of and invalidate the
provenance it would appear to tidy. Date records project-local, label wall-clock
instants KST when quoting them, and leave the commit metadata alone.

The three are equally *registered* and not equally *verifiable*, and a row that
says "attested" is making a claim about the evidence, not about the document's
honesty. `A1_PROVENANCE_LEDGER_2026-08-28.md`'s `F16` argues that reading at
