## The registered secondary reading is where the information is

The pre-registration also fixed *"the harm-vs-worker-count curve … the paper must
state the worker count at which it turns on."* It never turns on:

| arm | F's stream rate | harm |
|---|--:|--:|
| V+F1 | 0.34 GB/s | 1.0991 |
| V+F8 | 2.70 GB/s | 1.1202 |
| V+F16 | 5.40 GB/s | 1.1087 |

**F's stream rate spans 16×; the harm spans 1.019×.** It is flat. And the fused
tenant at **0.34 GB/s** harms V *more* (1.099×) than a dedicated streamer at
**23.3 GB/s** does (1.028×) — 68× the bandwidth, less harm.

A stream-rate effect cannot be flat in stream rate. Whatever F does to V, it is
not streaming that does it. Because these are comparisons *among F
configurations* rather than against an absolute harm threshold, this reading
survives the control's failure in a way the primary does not — but it is
suggestive, not conclusive, and the mechanism is tested separately in M1b.

## The candidate, stated as unregistered

What is constant across F1/F8/F16 is F's **hot table: 256 MiB**, instantiated
regardless of worker count. F(256 MiB) + V(170 MB) = **426 MiB against a 320 MiB
LLC**. So the obvious candidate is that F displaces V through its **reuse
structure**, not its stream.

**If that is right it is adverse to the three-party reframing**, and squarely so:
the remedy the frame proposes is a *stream-scoped* label, and F's hot table is
F's own working data, which must stay write-back. A stream-scoped mechanism —
H2 included — would not protect V from F. The middle row would have a
stakeholder and no remedy.

This inference is **not registered** and is not claimed here. `M1b` tests it
directly.
