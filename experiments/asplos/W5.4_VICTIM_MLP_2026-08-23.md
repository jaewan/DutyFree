# W5.4 -- Victim-side MLP: why the magnitudes are small, and which victims are harmable

Status: drafted as a `~/DutyFree` document. **Nothing has been written to
`~/STREAMING_Paper/`.** Placement is a S9 lead decision and is held.

No new measurement. Sources: `benchmarks/e2e/hnsw/HNSW_CAT_SENSITIVITY_OUTCOME.md`
(2026-08-21) and `benchmarks/e2e/gapbs/GAPBS_CAT_SENSITIVITY_OUTCOME.md`, both
committed. The result currently lives as a paragraph in `E2E_STATUS.md:93-99`
under the heading "a quantitative by-product". It is not a by-product. It is
the answer to the objection a reviewer will raise first, and it is also a
constraint on what this paper is allowed to claim. Both halves have to travel
together or the promotion is dishonest.

---

## The measurement

HNSW, `hnswlib` d9b3608 (v0.9.0), 1M float32 x d128 vectors, `M=16`, `efC=100`,
a 660,565,344-byte index, 10,000 `searchKnn` at `ef=64`, `k=10`; seven trials,
first discarded, six measured, three invocations per mask. CMT occupancy
verified against the granted mask on every host. No streamer, no aggressor:
this is a **capacity-sensitivity gate**, not a co-run.

| host | LLC | grant | DRAM traffic, 6 trials | traffic ratio | runtime ratio | **runtime / traffic** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mos181` (8592+) | 320 MiB / 20 | 320 MiB (305 occ) | 14.5 GB | 1.00 | 1.000 | -- |
| | | 16 MiB (15 occ) | 122.7 GB | **8.44** | **1.543** | **0.18** |
| `mos182` (8462Y+) | 60 MiB / 15 | 60 MiB (59 occ) | 77.2 GB | 1.00 | 1.000 | -- |
| | | 4 MiB (3 occ) | 142.1 GB | 1.84 | 1.325 | 0.72 |
| `moscxl` (9754) | 16 MiB/CCX / 16 | 16 MiB (14 occ) | 166.7 GB | 1.00 | 1.000 | -- |
| | | 1 MiB (1 occ) | 200.4 GB | 1.20 | 1.257 | 1.05 |

**The claim, in one sentence: capacity can remove 8.4x of a victim's DRAM
traffic and return only 1.5x in time.**

The victim is not insensitive to cache because the cache does nothing for it --
it demonstrably does a great deal, 122.7 GB down to 14.5 GB. It is insensitive
because its misses *overlap*. HNSW issues many independent proximity-graph
hops; the extra misses created by denying it cache are absorbed by
memory-level parallelism rather than paid for in time.

This is the same mechanism that explained the gem5 fused null (task #22),
observed here on silicon, with the traffic **measured** rather than inferred.
That is the part worth promoting: an explanation that was a hypothesis in
simulation now has a direct hardware measurement behind it.

## The selection criterion it yields

A capacity-sensitive victim needs **two** properties, not one:

1. reuse in the size range the mechanism can act on, **and**
2. limited ability to overlap the misses that removing that reuse creates.

HNSW has the first and not the second. Its upper proximity-graph layers do
reuse heavily -- but they are a few hundred KB, so they survive even a 1 MiB
mask, while the 630 MB of vector data below them is traversed too sparsely for
LLC capacity to matter. `../E2E_SESSION_PROMPT.md` S4.2 chose HNSW precisely
because it "reuses its upper layers heavily"; the reuse is real and in the
wrong size range for CAT to act on. PageRank at g21 on `moscxl` has both
properties, and it is the only victim configuration in the whole campaign to
pass a capacity-sensitivity gate (2.580x).

**The criterion is about the pairing, not the host, and there is an inversion
that proves it.** For PageRank, `mos181` was the *least* sensitive host
(1.311x at g21, and it failed the complete pre-registered g21-g25 range) and
`moscxl` the only passing one (2.580x). For HNSW the ordering inverts:
`mos181` is the *most* sensitive (1.544x) and `moscxl` the least (1.257x).
Neither host is "the one that absorbs interference". Sensitivity is a property
of victim x host, and the convenient summary -- "Intel absorbs it" -- is wrong.

## The other edge, which must be stated in the same breath

The same physics bounds this paper's own numbers. A shared-cache tax requires a
victim whose misses serialise; **bandwidth saved is not time saved**. Streaming
prevents an allocation; preventing an allocation returns time only to the
extent the victim cannot overlap the misses it prevents. The e2e magnitudes are
small for a reason that is now measured, and that reason applies to Streaming
exactly as it applies to CAT.

Promoting W5.4 therefore does two things at once, and a reviewer will see both
whether or not the paper says them:

- it **pre-empts** "why are your magnitudes small" with a measured mechanism
  rather than an apology, and
- it **caps** the magnitudes any mechanism of this class can deliver on a
  high-MLP victim, this one included.

Writing only the first half would be the kind of selective presentation S6.6
exists to forbid. The honest framing is that the paper found a real limit on
its own class of mechanism and reports it: *the benefit is bounded by the
victim's inability to overlap, and here is the measurement that bounds it*.

## What this licenses downstream

- **W7 gets its design rule.** "Build a fused variant with a genuinely
  serialising probe chain interleaved with a bandwidth-saturating stream" is
  W5.4's criterion turned into an experiment: property (2) has to be
  engineered in deliberately, because the natural workloads tried so far do not
  have it. W5.4 is the justification for why W7 is a legitimate experiment and
  not a fishing expedition -- the serialising chain is not chosen to inflate a
  number, it is chosen because the criterion says it is the regime where the
  mechanism can act at all, and the criterion was derived from a null.
- **It explains, without excusing, the e2e hunt's outcome.** Five workload
  families were tried and one configuration passed a gate. W5.4 says why, and
  says it in a form that predicts rather than rationalises.
- **It is the honest reply to "just use CAT".** CAT on the one passing
  configuration is exactly the L5 story of W5.3; W5.4 explains why the set of
  victims where any of this matters is smaller than the paper originally
  assumed.

## What this does not establish

- **The three-point ladder (1.05 -> 0.72 -> 0.18) is confounded with host.**
  One point per machine, three different microarchitectures and memory systems.
  It is monotone in LLC size, but there is no within-host ladder, so "larger
  caches return less of the traffic they save" is a suggestion the data is
  consistent with, not a demonstration. Presenting it as a curve would
  overstate it. The single-host claim -- 8.44x traffic, 1.54x time, on
  `mos181` -- carries the argument by itself and should be the one quoted.
- It says nothing about Streaming's benefit directly. It is a statement about
  what capacity does for one victim, measured with CAT and no co-runner.
- The HNSW index is **not byte-identical across hosts**, contrary to its own
  pre-registration: `-march=native` selects different AVX-512 forms on Zen 4
  than on SPR/EMR, producing two digests across three hosts. Disclosed, not
  repaired. It cannot carry the verdict -- the two hosts that *share* an index
  differ by more (1.325x vs 1.544x) than any construction difference could
  account for -- but it must be disclosed wherever the number is used.
- `mos181` and `mos182` traffic is `mbm_local_bytes` and is sound here
  (monotone, ~27.8 GB per trial, restarting near zero per group). The **GAPBS**
  gate's MBM is *not* usable -- stale counters under RMID recycling across 18
  groups -- so no traffic ratio exists for PageRank and none should be quoted.
  The PageRank comparison above is on runtime ratios only.
