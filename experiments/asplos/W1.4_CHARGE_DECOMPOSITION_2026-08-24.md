# W1.4 — why H2 removes 91% of the charge at an infinite SF and 0.5% at a finite one

Written 2026-08-24. **Re-analysis of surviving artifacts; zero compute cost.**
Evidence for edit-queue row 21, and a correction to the mechanism sentence at
`Sec5_Evaluation.tex:216-219`.

## The question

`tab:h3sf` shows H2 at 2.512x against WB's 2.501x under a finite snoop filter —
no effect — while W1 shows H2 at 1.034x against WB's 1.369x under an infinite
one — 90.9% of the charge removed. The paper explains the first by asserting the
charges are additive and H2 pays only one of them:

> H2 pays down capacity and leaves that tax where write-back left it
> (2.51$\times$). — `Sec5_Evaluation.tex:216-219`

That cannot be right as arithmetic. If H2 paid down the 1.369x capacity charge
and left the back-invalidation charge alone, the finite-SF total would fall well
below 2.501x. It does not fall at all. Something else is happening, and it is
worth knowing what, because this is the paragraph that motivates H3.

## Apparatus

Two campaigns, both already on disk, all arms n=3:

- infinite-SF arms: W1, `sf_{qui,wb,h2}_inf_s{1,2,3}`, gem5 `356e7b7d0e`,
  launcher `experiments/asplos/sf_inf_cells.sh` (committed).
- finite-SF arms: the 2026-08-20 remeasure behind `tab:h3sf`,
  `sf_{wb,h2,h3}_fin_s{1,2,3}`. **Launcher never committed (F10);** these
  survive only because `/tmp` outlived the campaign. Same geometry, verified
  from `config.ini`: victim `dutyfree/victim 2650 3000000`, aggressor
  `dutyfree/aggressor 16.0 [stream]`, 2 CPUs, L2 2 MiB/16-way, HNF 5 MiB/20-way,
  SF 65,536 entries when finite.

Every cyc/access below reproduces the published `tab:h3sf` row to within
0.09 cyc/access, so this is the same data the table reports.

Quiescent baseline: 101,644,349 cycles over 713,948 victim L2 demand misses =
**142.4 cycles per victim L2 demand miss**. Everything is measured against that.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

## What the paper's sentence gets wrong

**1. The charge is not mostly extra misses.** The natural reading of "a
back-invalidation tax" is that the victim suffers more misses. It does — 213,054
of them, +29.8% — but they are the minority of the cost. The finite SF also
inflates the cost of the misses the victim was always going to take, from 142.4
to 274.3 cycles each. Attribution between the two is order-dependent because
they interact:

| ordering | extra misses | per-miss inflation |
|---|---:|---:|
| extra misses charged at the baseline rate first | 30.3M (20%) | 122.3M (80%) |
| inflation charged on the baseline miss count first | 58.4M (38%) | 94.2M (62%) |

Under either ordering the per-miss inflation is the larger part: **62–80% of the
152.6M-cycle finite-SF charge**. Both orderings are reported because neither is
privileged; quoting only one would be picking the flattering split.

**2. H2 is not "paying down capacity and leaving the rest."** Per-miss inflation
is *the same quantity* H2 removes 90.9% of at an infinite SF. At a finite SF it
removes 0.5% of it. H2 has not been restricted to one of two additive charges —
its own charge has stopped being removable.

**3. The reason is that back-invalidation destroys the resource H2 protects.**
The victim alone hits the LLC 99.99% of the time; it is an LLC-resident chase by
construction. WB contention drops that to 20.8%, and H2's job is to give it back
— it does, to 43.7%. Under a finite SF the LLC demand hit rate is **0.17%**.

That 0.17% is aggregate over both cores, so it bounds the victim's own LLC hit
rate from above: 6,668 aggregate demand hits against 927,002 victim L2 misses
means the victim hits the LLC on **at most 0.7%** of its misses. There is no
residency left for H2 to protect. H2's exclusion is still working — it lifts the
aggregate hit rate to 8.76% and cuts HNF demand misses 11.5% — but from a floor
where it cannot pay.

**4. H2 does not reduce SF pressure at all, which is why.** HNF inbound
transitions, summed over the run:

| | WB inf | H2 inf | WB fin | H2 fin | H2+H3 fin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `SF_Eviction` | 0 | 0 | 2,511,466 | 2,505,870 | **6,761** |
| `LocalHN_Eviction` | 4,424,887 | 31,669 | 6,466,212 | 379 | 37,329 |
| `ReadOnce` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5,612,431 |

H2 changes SF evictions by **−0.2%**. Its streaming lines still enrol, exactly
as the paper says. H3 removes 99.7% of them. The 5.6M `ReadOnce` transitions
appearing only under H3 confirm the realization is variant (a), no-retention —
which is edit-queue row 6's point, now with a counter behind it.

## The corrected sentence

Not "H2 pays down capacity and leaves that tax where write-back left it," but:

> A finite SF does not add a second, separable tax on top of the capacity tax.
> It removes the victim's LLC residency outright — the hit rate falls from 99.99%
> alone to 0.17% — and a capacity fix cannot help a victim that has no capacity
> left to defend. H2 still excludes the stream and still enrols its own lines,
> so the 2.5M snoop-filter evictions are unchanged (−0.2%) and the victim's cost
> per miss is unchanged (−0.5%). Only declining enrolment removes them, and once
> they are gone the capacity charge becomes removable again: H2+H3 lands at
> 151.1 cycles per miss against H2-alone's 147.1 at an infinite SF.

Same conclusion — only H3 removes it — reached by a mechanism that survives
arithmetic.

## A second, smaller correction

`tab:h3sf` reports 2.501x and 2.512x, and the surrounding text treats them as
the same number ("indistinguishable at ±0.07"). With n=3 they are not:

- WB, finite SF: 84.7541 ± 0.0698 (1 sd)
- H2, finite SF: 85.0959 ± 0.0173 (1 sd)
- difference **+0.3418 cyc/access, 4.8 sd**

H2 is reproducibly **0.40% worse** than write-back under a finite snoop filter.
The magnitude is negligible and the paper's conclusion is untouched, but the
claim of indistinguishability is not supported by its own data, and the honest
statement is "H2 costs a further 0.4%, consistent with paying the exclusion's
own overhead while buying nothing." The published half-range of ≤0.07 was
computed per cell and does not bound a difference between cells.

## Limits

1. The finite-SF arms are from an **uncommitted launcher** (F10). Their geometry
   was verified from the surviving `config.ini`, not from a runner in git. If
   any of the finite/infinite comparison ever becomes load-bearing beyond a
   mechanism explanation, it should be re-run from `sf_inf_cells.sh` with
   `HNF_SF_FINITE=1`, which is a ~2h job.
2. The two campaigns are four days and one gem5 commit apart. The infinite arms
   are `356e7b7d0e`; the finite arms are from the 2026-08-20 remeasure, whose
   recorded commit (`0f37c28`) is wrong and cannot have produced them (W1
   provenance finding 2), so the finite arms' commit is *inferred* to be the
   same `356e7b7d0e`, not established.
3. LLC hit rates are aggregate over both cores. The 0.17% bound on the victim is
   sound because aggregate hits are fewer than victim misses; the 43.73% and
   40.45% figures are not attributable to either core and are not used for
   anything load-bearing here.
4. All of this is one geometry — 2650 KiB victim, 5 MiB/20-way LLC, 65,536-entry
   SF. `tab:sens` shows the infinite-SF recovery is flat (88.8–91.9%) across
   assoc and WSS; nothing here shows the finite-SF collapse is.
