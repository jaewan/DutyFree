# W1.3 — the first variance estimate for a gem5 number in this project

Written 2026-08-23, from W1's comparator cells, while the decisive H2/infinite-SF
cells were still running. No new compute was spent: the six runs already existed.

## The gap this closes

`GATE1_FUSED_NULL_CORRECTION_2026-08-15.md` §6 names a systemic defect:

> **no gem5 number in this project currently has a variance estimate**,
> including `tab:gem5`, `tab:sens` and `tab:h1bw`.

§6.1 then amends the proposed remedy. An 18-run `SEED` sweep looked like a
working variance mechanism (CoV 0.023–0.34%), but a control at a **fixed** seed
with byte-identical instantiated config did not reproduce its counterpart —
562,510 vs 562,777 HNF fills, 0.047% drift, the same magnitude as the
cross-seed spread at that cell (0.043%). So the variation is run-to-run
nondeterminism, not a seed-controlled quantity. The prescribed estimator is
therefore **repeated identical runs**, not a seed sweep.

## W1's comparator cells are that estimator, by accident of naming

W1 runs `sf_*_inf_s{1,2,3}` with `SEED=1,2,3` and `RUBY_RANDOMIZATION=1`. Per
§6.1 the `SEED` value is **not a treatment** here: setting `SEED` enables
divergence but does not control it. What the three runs actually are is three
independent executions of an identical configuration with randomisation on —
which is exactly what §6.1 asks for. The `_s1/_s2/_s3` suffixes are naming
inherited from the original `tab:h3sf` batch, and nothing downstream should
report them as seed sensitivity.

Registered here so that the distinction is on the record before the H2 and
H2+H3 cells land.

## The estimate

Metric: `system.cpu0.numCycles / 3e6` (§5.3 ITERS gate), victim
`dutyfree/victim 2650 3000000`, aggressor `dutyfree/aggressor 16.0 stream`,
gem5 `356e7b7d0e`, `HNF_SF_FINITE=0`.

| cell | s1 | s2 | s3 | mean | sd | CoV | range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| quiescent / infinite SF | 33.865 | 33.891 | 33.888 | 33.8813 | 0.0142 | **0.042%** | 0.026 |
| WB / infinite SF | 46.365 | 46.343 | 46.432 | 46.3800 | 0.0464 | **0.100%** | 0.089 |

Propagating both into the published ratio:

> **WB/infinite-SF tax = 1.3689 ± 0.0015 (1 sd), 95% CI ±0.0029.**

This is the first gem5 number in the project carried with an uncertainty.
0.042–0.100% sits inside the 0.023–0.34% family §6.1 measured on a different
kernel and geometry, which is weak corroboration that the magnitude is a
property of the simulator's nondeterminism rather than of this workload.

## What it does for W1's verdict

W1's pre-registered bands are PASS ≤1.10×, PARTIAL 1.10–1.30×, FAIL ≥1.30×. The
nearest boundary is **67 standard deviations** from 1.00×. Whatever the
H2/infinite-SF cells read, the verdict cannot be a noise call, and n=3 is
already more than the decision needs. No additional repetitions are warranted —
which is the same conclusion §6 reached the expensive way, when RUN2 of the
2 MiB and 16 MiB arms cost ~1.5 h × 4 cores and returned bit-identical numbers.

It does **not** license reading small differences. W1.2's consistency check on
H2+H3 vs H2 uses a ±5% band; that is ~46 sd, so it too is safe. But a future
claim resting on, say, a 0.3% gem5 difference is inside the noise and must not
be made from n=3.

## Limits, stated so the number is not over-used

1. **n=3 estimates a standard deviation badly.** The 90% interval on an sd from
   three samples spans roughly 0.6×–2.9× the point estimate. Read 0.1% as
   "order 0.1%", not as 0.100%.
2. **The three runs were launched concurrently on one host.** They share
   thermal, scheduler and memory-system conditions, so this is within-batch
   nondeterminism and is a **lower bound** on the variance a re-run weeks later
   would show.
3. **CoV is not portable.** This estimate belongs to this cell, this geometry,
   this binary. §6.1's own spread across cells was an order of magnitude wide.
   `tab:gem5`, `tab:sens` and `tab:h1bw` still have no variance estimate; they
   need their own repeated runs, and the honest interim statement in the paper
   is that they have none.
4. **It says nothing about model fidelity.** A tight CoV means the simulator
   answers the same question consistently. W3.1 and F9.6 are both about the
   simulator being asked a question whose ratios are not the hardware's.

## Cost

Zero. Both cells were already required as W1 comparators.
