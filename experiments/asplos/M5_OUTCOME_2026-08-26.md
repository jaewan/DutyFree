`pointer_chase` 170 MB on cpu8, F = 16 workers at hit rate 1.0, fact 256 MiB on
CXL node 2, n=7, order rotated, 35/35 records.

## What this means

**Declining to allocate the stream removes 100% of the harm the stream causes.**
Not 28%. The 28% figure was the stream's share of *total* harm in a configuration
where the tenant *also* held a 256 MiB working set of its own — and that second
working set is what the flush could not touch.

The two-component model was right in structure and wrong in interpretation. The
components are not *residency* and *transport*. They are:

- **the stream's residency** — which a page-scoped label removes **completely**;
- **the tenant's own resident working set** — which no admission-control
  mechanism should remove, because the tenant is using it.

There is no unexplained congestion term on this platform. M4's rate-invariance
now makes sense in the same stroke: a 256 MiB table is 256 MiB at every worker
count, so a component made of it cannot scale with rate. **M4's "failure" was the
correct result of a mis-specified question.**

## W5.3's counter-point is resolved, without rate-dependence

`W5.3`'s Intel aggressor was a **pure stream with no reuse structure**, so its
harm was entirely stream residency — and a capacity control reached **1.00×**.
Exactly what M5 predicts. The contradiction that stood for two reports came from
comparing a bare streamer against a tenant carrying its own working set, and
attributing the difference to the mechanism instead of to the tenant.

Three of three Intel points now agree:

| configuration | tenant's own resident set | residency-only intervention reaches |
|---|---|---|
| W5.3, pure streamer | none | **1.00×** (CAT12) |
| M5, 4 MiB table | negligible | **0.993×** (flush-behind) |
| M3b/M4/M5, 256 MiB table | 256 MiB | 2.28–2.32× — the tenant's own set remains |

## The one point still outside the model

AMD's flush-behind arm left a **2.30×** floor with an aggressor that had no hot
table. On the Intel account that floor should have been ~1.0×. Either AMD's
residual is a genuinely different mechanism — plausible and independently
established, since **E3** found AMD's harm rate-class where Intel's is
capacity-class — or the AMD arm's victim contributed resident state the Intel
victim does not.

**Recorded as the remaining open point, not explained away.** It is one datum on
one unreachable machine, against three consistent Intel points, and the honest
statement is that the Intel account is complete and the AMD residual is
unattributed.

## Registered instrument check: two pass, one misses by 0.001

`Fbig_retain` 2.7795 vs M4's 2.7677 — OK. `Fbig_flush` 2.3150 vs 2.2793 — OK.
**`V` = 78.049 against a registered window of [78.05, 78.20] — a miss, by
0.001 cyc/load.**

Reported as a miss because it is one. The judgement, stated rather than hidden:
the deviation is 0.08 of the arm's own within-run sd (0.012) and 1 part in
78,000; the window existed to catch a *polluted* baseline, of the kind M1 had at
201.3, and it did that job. I am not voiding a 35-record run over 0.001, and I am
not pretending the check passed. The threshold was set one digit too tight.

## What the paper can now claim

> A page-granular, OS-declared memory type that declines shared-cache allocation
> for an immutable read stream removes **all** of the harm that stream inflicts on
> a co-resident latency-critical neighbour — measured at **100.5%** recovery, with
> the neighbour returned to **0.993×** of its solo performance. What remains in
> any given deployment is the streaming tenant's *own* resident working set, which
> is not the mechanism's to remove.

That is a materially different and stronger claim than "the mechanism buys 28%".
And it is the claim the registered test was built to falsify.

## What it does *not* fix — the frontier still stands

The mechanism removes the stream's contribution completely. It does **not**
return every neighbour to baseline in every configuration, because a tenant with
a large working set of its own still displaces its neighbour — and there, shipped
knobs (CAT+MBA) reach 1.00–1.07× by constraining the tenant's *whole* footprint,
at a measured +19–44% charge to the tenant's reuse structure.

So the trade is now sharply stated, and it is a better trade than yesterday's:

| approach | removes | leaves | cost to the tenant | tuning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **page-scoped label** | the stream's residency, **completely** | the tenant's own working set | **zero** | **none** |
| shipped knobs | the tenant's whole footprint | nothing | +19–44% on its reuse structure | per-vendor, state-dependent |

The label is the only mechanism that separates the two, which is the argument the
paper set out to make — and the first time in three days that a measurement has
supported it.
