# What is still needed before the paper can be written

Written 2026-08-28. The lead's instruction is that the paper gets crafted once the
results are in, so this document does the prior step: work out which results are
missing, which assessments have to happen regardless, and in what order.

Method: take the claim the paper now makes, and for each load-bearing number ask
**how many points is it measured at, and what would a reviewer vary?**

## The spine, as it now stands

The necessity argument is gone (L5 dead on both vendors, `W5.3`). What replaced it
is a **two-sided trade-off**, from M12:

| approach | neighbour | tenant pays | neighbour pays |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| do nothing | 2.258x | --- | --- |
| object label | 1.311x | ~0 | 0 |
| way mask (8 of 20) | 1.122x | 16.7% | 13.1% |
| both | 1.120x | 16.7% | 13.1% |

Plus two legs that do not depend on it: MBA's working point is a knife edge
(12.44x at a 28 GB/s cap, 1.08x at 24), and H3's coherence elision is a
capability no predictor can offer.

## The problem with the spine

**Every number in that table is measured at one point.** One way split (8/12), one
victim working set (170 MB), one tenant table (128 MiB), one stream size (1 GiB),
one hit rate, one victim type, one machine. Three of those numbers are now in the
abstract.

The reviewer's first question is not whether 13.1% is correct. It is **"what
happens at a different split?"** --- and we cannot answer it.

# Experiments

## E1 --- The allocation frontier. **The paper cannot ship without this.**

Sweep the way split and the victim's working set together, because they are the
same capacity budget seen from two sides. For each split $k$ (tenant gets $k$ of
20 ways, victim the complementary $20-k$), measure three things:

1. the victim's harm while the tenant runs,
2. the victim's **own confinement cost** --- victim alone at $20-k$ ways, which is
   the control M12's P5 proved indispensable,
3. the tenant's cost at $k$ ways.

That produces the Pareto frontier over static partitions, and it answers the
question the current single point cannot: **is there any split that protects the
neighbour without charging either party much?**

- Splits: $k \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16\}$.
- Victim WSS: $\{32, 96, 170\}$ MB --- so the 13.1% can be checked against
  occupancy of its own partition rather than asserted from the one tight fit
  (170 MB in 192 MiB is 84% full, and M8's lesson was that exactly this kind of
  tight fit produces the whole effect).
- Tenant table fixed at 128 MiB, stream 1 GiB, hit rate 0.5.

**Why this is the priority and not a refinement.** The mechanism is a fixed
capacity budget: the victim needs most of the cache to avoid conflict misses, the
tenant needs most of it to hold its table, and twenty ways cannot satisfy both. If
the frontier shows that, the claim gets *much* stronger than it is now ---
"no static partition satisfies both parties, because their demands sum to more
than the cache; a label removes one party's demand without partitioning at all."
If instead there is a comfortable split, **the trade-off claim collapses** and we
need to know that before writing, not after.

Cost: ~54 co-run cells plus controls at $n{=}10$; victim runs are ~13 s. Roughly
2--3 hours. Registration must state the CoV basis (victim CoV is 0.03--0.66%, so
$n{=}10$ resolves ~1%; tenant CoV at hit rate 0.5 is <=3.7%, so $n{=}10$ resolves
~8%).

## E2 --- Hold the stream fixed and sweep the tenant's footprint

The paper's decomposition table now carries a dagger admitting that its middle row
streams 1 GiB where the outer rows stream 256 MiB. That is an acknowledged
confound in a table a reviewer will read closely, and M11 showed stream size is
worth 15--21 points on the tenant's axis.

Sweep the tenant footprint at **one** stream size: $\{4, 16, 64, 128, 256\}$ MiB
x $\{$retain, non-allocating$\}$, no mask, $n{=}10$. Turns three points with a
confound into one clean sweep, and it is the sweep the two-component account
actually claims.

Cost: 20 cells, ~40 min.

## E3 --- A second victim, and ideally a second machine

Everything on the neighbour side is one `pointer_chase` --- a latency-bound random
walk, i.e. the most sensitive victim that exists. The paper claims things about
"a neighbour". Two cheap options:

- **`oltp_index`/gprobe**, which is W5.3's own victim, so the result is
  cross-checkable against an existing campaign; and/or
- **mos182 (SPR 8462Y+)**, a different LLC geometry (60 MiB / 15 ways), which also
  tests whether the frontier's shape is a property of 20x16 MiB or of caches.

Re-run only the decisive M12 cells --- unmitigated, label, mask --- not the frontier.

Cost: ~1 hour per victim/host.

## E4 --- The duty-cycle claim, if we want it to be empirical

The paper flags that a mask charges the neighbour continuously while the label
charges it only during the stream, and derives a 42% break-even arithmetically
with the deployment assumption stated. Measuring it is straightforward --- run the
tenant at several duty cycles and record the victim's time-averaged slowdown under
each mechanism.

**Optional.** The arithmetic version with its assumption named is defensible. Only
worth doing if E1 leaves room in the page budget, which it probably will not.

## E5 --- AMD's narrow-aggressor cell

## E6 --- `tab:gem5`'s +H2 column

# Assessments (no machine time, and two of them are mandatory)

## A1 --- A fresh provenance ledger. **Mandatory before any writing.**

`W4.3_PROVENANCE_LEDGER_2026-08-23.md` predates: four withdrawals, the S1-1
retraction, M6--M12, and three rewritten tables. Every number currently in the
paper needs re-binding to a surviving artifact, and the ledger's own verdict
column needs re-running. This is the step that would have caught S1-1.

## A2 --- A quantity index. **Mandatory, and cheap.**

The failure that produced both the L5 error and the S1-1 retraction was the same:
quoting a committed number without checking for a later document that supersedes
it. M3b and M5 are adjacent files with contradictory titles.

The remedy is one table: **for each quantity the paper cites, the latest document
that measures it**, with superseded documents explicitly marked. Half a day, and
it retires a failure mode that has cost us three times this week.

## A3 --- Consolidate the proxy-validity account

What `clflushopt` flush-behind does and does not model is currently spread across
M3, M3b (whose interpretation is retracted), M5, M9, M12b and M12c, one of which
has an unfit instrument. A reviewer who pulls that thread finds six documents and
a retraction. It needs to be one statement: what the proxy over-states, what it
under-states, the 32x distance sweep that bounds it, and the fact that the
neighbour-side measurement (M12b's 75%) is the well-conditioned one while the
tenant-side cost axis is unusable.

## A4 --- Can H3 carry weight with no silicon evidence?

H3 is gem5-only by construction --- no snoop filter we can reach levies the
enrolment charge it removes, and it costs 3.45% over H2 alone where there is no
charge to remove. It is a genuine ISA ask and the one thing no predictor can
offer. **Lead call:** does it stay a first-class contribution, or become a
discussion section? This affects the page budget materially.

## A5 --- Is L1's evidence strong enough?

After the RocksDB 54% deletion, "the demand exists" rests on a *source* exhibit:
`fill_cache=false` reaching `no_insert`, and the SST writer calling
`POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED`. That shows shipped software hand-rolls both halves, which
is real but is not a measured application benefit. `duckdb_join`, `hnsw` and
`gapbs` are in the tree. **Assessment:** accept the source exhibit and say plainly
that we measure no application speedup, or spend time on one.

## A6 --- Page budget and venue, together

Body is 15 pages; the norm is 11--13. Nothing added this week was decorative ---
every item is a scope condition, a withdrawal, or a price --- so cutting means
deciding which arguments survive a page limit. That is the same decision as the
venue choice, which has been open a week. **These should be made as one call, and
made before the paper is crafted, not after.**
