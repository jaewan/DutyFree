# M3b outcome: ~27% of the harm is residency; ~73% is bytes in flight and no admission-control mechanism can reach it

Registered in `M3_FLUSHBEHIND_PREREG_2026-08-25.md` Addendum 1 (`4ea89ec`),
specified before the run. mos181, victim `pointer_chase` 170 MB on cpu8,
F = 16 fused workers, hit rate 1.0, **n=7 with exact position balance** (each arm
appears once in each of 7 positions), 49/49 records.

| arm | n | victim cyc/load | CoV | harm | recovery |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| V alone | 7 | 78.052 | 0.02% | 1.0000 | — |
| V+F, read **and retain** | 7 | 216.553 | 0.54% | 2.7745 | — |
| V+F, flush behind **32 KiB** | 7 | 179.865 | 0.43% | 2.3044 | **26.4%** |
| V+F, flush behind **64 KiB** | 7 | 179.458 | 0.34% | 2.2992 | **26.7%** |
| V+F, flush behind **256 KiB** | 7 | 177.640 | 0.22% | 2.2759 | **28.0%** |
| V+F, flush behind **1 MiB** | 7 | 185.768 | 0.23% | 2.3801 | **22.1%** |
| V+F, **not read at all** | 7 | 77.561 | 0.01% | 0.9937 | — |

## The registered verdict: FLAT

Recovery across a **32× range** of flush distances: 26.4 / 26.7 / 28.0 / 22.1 —
a spread of **5.8 percentage points**, inside the registered ±10 threshold. The
branch that fires is:

> **~27% is the real residency share. The remaining ~73% of the harm is bytes in
> flight, and is unreachable by any admission-control mechanism, H2 included.**

The curve's shape is exactly what that reading predicts. Tightening the residency
bound from 256 KiB to 32 KiB — an 8× reduction in how much of the stream can be
resident — changes the neighbour's cost by **1.2%**. If residency were the
mechanism, squeezing it 8× harder would have helped substantially. It does
nothing. Meanwhile relaxing to 1 MiB *does* lose ground (28.0% → 22.1%), which
confirms the instrument is sensitive to residency in the direction it should be —
it simply runs out of harm to remove at around a quarter.

So this is not a null from a blunt instrument. The instrument responds correctly
when residency is allowed to grow, and stops responding when residency is already
small, which is the signature of a bound that has been reached rather than an
effect that was never there.

## What is now established

A fused tenant degrades a latency-critical neighbour **2.77×**, reproduced across
four campaigns with the baseline landing at 78.05–78.18 every time. Of that:

- **~27% is cache residency** — the stream's lines occupying the shared cache.
  This is the part an admission-control mechanism can address: H2, a staging
  buffer, or CAT.
- **~73% is transport** — memory-controller queues, fabric and CXL occupancy,
  bank contention. None of it cares whether the line is retained.

## Why every earlier result now fits

This is the first result today that *explains* the others rather than correcting
them:

- **W5.3's AMD row.** CAT alone (a capacity control) left a 12.4× residual; only
  CAT **+ MBA** (capacity *and* bandwidth) reached 1.07×. If harm were mostly
  residency, capacity partitioning alone should have sufficed. It did not, and a
  27/73 split is why.
- **The AMD CAT residual.** Way-partitioning removes 53% of that tax and leaves
  9.87×. A mechanism acting only on residency cannot close a transport-dominated
  gap.
- **The T4 gate.** The fused tax was 15.9% memory-bound. Same story from the
  other end: most of what a stream costs is not where a cache mechanism acts.
- **The MLP law.** Victims whose misses overlap absorb residency pressure but not
  transport pressure, which is why elasticity varied 0.18–1.05 across hosts.

## What it means for the paper

The mechanism is not useless and it is not the answer. Quantified:

> Declining to allocate an immutable read stream removes about **a quarter** of
> the harm that stream does to a co-resident latency-critical neighbour. The
> other three quarters is transport, which no admission-control mechanism — ours
> or anyone's — can reach. On the case where no shipped label can express the
> policy at all (one thread, two access classes), that quarter is available at
> **zero new architectural state**; the software proxy that achieves it today
> costs the streamer 14–17% of its own throughput.

That is a real, defensible, quantitatively bounded contribution. It is also
decisively **not** "STREAMING fixes cache pollution", and the paper must not be
written as though a quarter were the whole.

## Caveats, unchanged and honest

- `clflushopt` evicts lines already allocated; a hardware H2 would never allocate
  them. So 27% is a **lower bound**, though the flatness across 32× argues the
  true figure is close — there is little residency left to remove at 32 KiB.
- mos181 only (Intel EMR, 320 MiB non-inclusive LLC). AMD unreachable for two
  days. AMD's harm is rate-class rather than capacity-class (E3), so the split
  could differ there — and W5.3's CAT+MBA result suggests it would be *more*
  transport-dominated, not less.
- The victim metric saturates (78 → ~209), which is why this rests on a swept
  curve rather than a single point.
- One thread count (16 workers), one victim, one geometry.

# Addendum 1 — 2026-08-25: the panel is right on five counts, and the reconciliation is better than either of us said

The body above is left verbatim. **Its claim sentence is withdrawn**, one
retrodiction is struck, a counter-retrodiction is added, and the arm identity it
was missing is attached.

## A1 — "about a quarter" is withdrawn. It is a point on a curve, not a yield.

The panel is correct and I verified it rather than accepting it. The AMD
flush-behind arm is the **same quantity, same instrument, same 256 KiB
distance**, computed by the same formula:

| | allocating | flush-behind | not read | recovery |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| AMD Bergamo (hash-join victim, n=12) | 6.4840× | 2.3020× | 1.0000× | **76.3%** |
| Intel EMR (pointer chase, n=7) | 2.7745× | 2.2759× | 0.9937× | **28.0%** |

So the residency share is **operating-point dependent across at least 28–76%**,
and quoting 28% as *the* yield was wrong. My caveat paragraph compounded it by
arguing AMD "would be more transport-dominated", with a residency-*dominant* AMD
result sitting in the same project. That was a self-contradiction a referee finds
in one read.

**But the two points reconcile better than a range suggests.** Look at the
post-flush residuals rather than the fractions:

- AMD after flushing: **2.3020×**
- Intel after flushing: **2.2759×**
- difference: **1.1%**

The recovery fractions differ 2.7× *only because the allocating harm differed*
(6.48× vs 2.77×). Residency contributed 4.18× on AMD and 0.50× on Intel — on top
of a **near-identical non-residency floor of ≈2.3×**.

That is a much stronger statement than either percentage: **flush-behind lands
both platforms on the same floor.** Whatever the non-residency component is, it
appears to be set by something common to both configurations rather than by the
vendor.

Stated with the caution it needs: these are **different victims** (hash-join
probe vs pointer chase), different vendors and different cache geometries, so a
1.1% agreement between two numbers could be coincidence. It is an observation
worth one line and a test, not a law. **The rate sweep is the test** — if the
floor is queueing near saturation, it should move with rate, and if it is
fill-path work it should not.

## A2 — retrodiction #3 is struck

The T4 gate's "15.9% memory-bound" decomposed the Δ that the hit-rate finding
subsequently showed was a workload artifact (hit-rate mismatch plus a hardware
`div`). **Citing the decomposition of a tax that does not exist as evidence for
the transport model is invalid.** Struck. The other three retrodictions stand and
cost nothing.

## A3 — W5.3's Intel row is a counter-retrodiction, and it was omitted

On Intel SPR, **CAT12 alone — a pure capacity control — took the victim from
1.62× to 1.00×** at 0.7% streamer cost, and W5.3 records that MBA bought "9% of
the excess, and 0% once CAT is on". At that operating point Intel harm was
**entirely residency**, which a flat "73% of Intel harm is transport" cannot
survive.

The available reconciliation is that the non-residency share is **rate- or
magnitude-dependent**: W5.3's Intel point is a 1.62× harm, today's is 2.77×, and
queueing at a shared bandwidth resource is nonlinear near saturation. That
rescues the model and makes it predictive — **but it is currently invoked, not
shown**, and selective retrodiction is the exact failure this project has caught
in itself repeatedly. It is registered as the thing the sweep must demonstrate,
and until it does, the model is a hypothesis with three supporting points and one
unexplained counter-point, and must be written that way.

## A4 — "transport" is a residual with a hypothesised name

What survives flushing is everything the stream does *other than retain lines*:
memory-controller and link occupancy, yes — but also fill-path and lookup work,
the transient residency inside the flush window, and `clflushopt`'s own coherence
traffic. Calling it "transport" asserts a mechanism the measurement does not
establish. **Renamed to "the non-residency component" throughout until the sweep
identifies it.**

The panel's observation that this is the same open question in two costumes is
correct and worth carrying: it is the Intel face of the unattributed AMD 9.87×
CAT residual, and of the hash-join campaign's unexplained remainder.

## A5 — the arm identity, attached

Missing from the body, at the clause the claim depends on. The streamer in M3/M3b
was:

**The fused tenant itself** — `cxl_join_bench --mode morsel`, **16 workers** on
cores 32–47, 256 MiB fact array on CXL node 2, 256 MiB hot table on node 0,
1 MiB morsels, **`--hit-rate 1.0`**, streaming at **8.482 GB/s**. Victim:
`pointer_chase`, **170 MB**, single thread on **cpu8**, same socket and same LLC,
hugepage-backed, node 0.

Two consequences.

**The three-party premise holds, decisively.** A fused tenant at its own pace
harms a latency-critical neighbour **2.77×**, far above the 1.15× falsifier
registered in M1. It also holds at the tenant's *natural* hit rate of 0.5
(2.68×, M1b/M2), so this is not an artifact of the hit-rate lever. The clause
attaches.

**But `--hit-rate 1.0` is not the tenant's natural operating point.** It was
chosen because M2 showed that at 0.5 the table's miss-scatter independently
saturates the victim and masks the stream entirely. So the 28% figure is measured
at a *deliberately unmasked* operating point, with F streaming 8.482 GB/s rather
than its natural 5.217. That belongs beside the number every time it is quoted.

## A6 — the frontier, stated honestly

The panel's uncomfortable consequence is correct and the body stopped short of
it: **in no measured configuration does non-allocation alone return a neighbour
to baseline.**

| approach | leaves the victim at | cost to the streaming tenant | tuning | works where no CLOS can aim |
|---|--:|---|---|---|
| label proxy (flush-behind) | **2.28×** (Intel), **2.30×** (AMD) | 14–17% here, 31.34% on AMD | none | **yes** |
| shipped knobs (CAT, +MBA) | **1.00–1.07×** | +19–44% on F's reuse structure (`tab:fused`) | per-vendor, and state-dependent | no |

Neither dominates. The label removes the residency share at zero cost to the
tenant and zero tuning, in the one configuration nothing shipped can address at
all; the knobs remove nearly everything but charge the tenant's own working set
and need per-vendor tuning. **That is a trade, not a rescue, and it is the honest
figure.** Written as a fix, §A1's contradiction sinks it.

## What replaces the withdrawn claim

> Neighbour harm from an immutable read stream decomposes into a **residency**
> component, which declining to allocate removes, and a **non-residency**
> component, which it does not. The residency share is operating-point dependent
> and measured between **28% (Intel EMR, 2.77× harm) and 76% (AMD Bergamo,
> 6.48× harm)** with one instrument at one flush distance; both platforms land on
> a post-flush floor near **2.3×**. A page-scoped label buys the residency
> component at zero new architectural state, zero tenant cost and zero tuning, in
> the one configuration no context-scoped label can express — and composes with
> rate control for the remainder.

## Registered next, and it is the last measurement before writing

**Aggressor rate × {retain, flush-256 KiB}, fixed victim, Intel, ~5 rate points.**
It reconciles A1 and A3 in one experiment: if the post-flush floor scales with
bytes/s and collapses under a modest rate cut near saturation, the non-residency
component is queueing, the residency share becomes a **curve**, and today's 28%,
W5.3's Intel 100%, and AMD's 76% land on one axis. If the floor is rate-invariant,
it is fill-path work and the model needs a different second component.

Opportunistic addition: gem5 under-predicted the silicon WB tax by 39% and its
MC/link queues are explicit and inspectable. If the model holds, that gap should
be visible as under-modelled queueing. An afternoon, and it turns a known
simulator defect into evidence.
