# W6.3 — the CAT comparison, and why the plan's version of it should not be made

Written 2026-08-24. Literature check, no compute.

`PLAN_B_REBUILD.md` W6.3 says: *"State the comparison honestly: CAT shipped for
less benefit than this."* **The evidence does not support that sentence and it
should not go in the paper.** What the evidence does support is a different and
stronger comparison, which this memo sets out.

## Why the plan's claim fails

Three ways, any one of which is fatal:

1. **Published CAT benefits are not small.** Intel's own PQoS/DPDK determinism
   study reports isolation restoring performance "up to 94%"; a third-party
   measurement on a Xeon D-1541 with a 12 MB L3 reports a noisy neighbour
   inflating a victim from ~5.0 to 6.5 µs — a 30% degradation — which CAT
   removes to within measurement noise of eliminating the neighbour entirely.
   Both are larger than the 6% e2e magnitudes Plan B is trying to justify.
2. **It contradicts our own result.** W5.3's two-vendor table says CAT
   *recovers* the Intel capacity charge on mos182. We cannot build contribution
   (2) on "each vendor's knob works on its own machine" and simultaneously argue
   CAT delivers little. The claim would be inconsistent within one paper.
3. **It is the wrong axis.** Cache QoS shipped on a determinism-and-capability
   argument, not a throughput one. Herdrich et al.'s stated contribution in the
   shipping paper (HPCA'16) is that prior shared-cache QoS work was
   simulation-and-analysis only and *no commercial platform had integrated such
   techniques into a practical solution* — a capability gap, closed. Nobody
   promised a mean speedup, so beating one is not the comparison.

Fishing for a reading of CAT's history that makes our number look adequate is
the §6.6 move, run on someone else's result instead of our own. Don't.

## The comparison that is true, and is better

**CAT enforces at exactly the point H2 enforces, on shipping Intel silicon.**
From the SDM (Vol. 3B §17, read at source):

> the fraction of cache space that a class of service can **allocate into**
>
> all requests to the CAT-capable resource from that logical processor are
> **tagged with that COS**
>
> The capacity bitmask may be mapped into a way bitmask (or a similar
> enforcement entity based on the implementation) [and applied to] the
> allocation policy

So the shipping architecture already: (i) carries a **per-request label** from
the core to the LLC, and (ii) consults that label **in the allocation decision**
on the fill path. That is H2's mechanism, structurally, and it is not a proposal
— it is in the SDM and in every Xeon since E5-2600 v3.

**This retires the weak version of W6.1's argument.** W6.1 proposes arguing
"against datapaths that already exist for WC and MOVNTDQA." Those are the wrong
precedent: they are about bypassing the hierarchy for a *store or load
instruction*. CAT is the right precedent, because it is a per-request admission
decision at the LLC fill point, which is the same decision H2 makes. Use CAT.

**What differs is not the mechanism but what the label can name.** CAT's tag is
the logical processor's Class of Service, bound at context switch and belonging
to a *thread*. \textsc{Streaming}'s is a page attribute, bound at translation
and belonging to an *object*. Same datapath position, same fill-path
consultation, different binding time and different scope. That sentence is the
paper's title and its contribution (2), stated as an architectural fact rather
than an aspiration — and it is the sharpest form of it we have.

## Footprint: the direction the plan wanted, on a defensible axis

CAT's architectural surface, as enumerated by the SDM:

- a new CPUID enumeration path — leaf 07H `EBX.PQE`, then leaf 10H with a
  sub-leaf per resource type, reporting bitmask length and number of classes
- a per-logical-processor association register (`IA32_PQR_ASSOC`) holding the COS
- a **file of mask MSRs**, `IA32_L3_MASK_n`, one per class of service, plus the
  L2 equivalents and an `IA32_L3_QOS_CFG` for CDP, which remaps the mask space
  1:2 and halves the usable class count
- an architectural validity rule on mask contents — "All (and only) contiguous
  1's in the CBM are permitted" — that software must respect
- an OS obligation on **every context switch**: write the incoming thread's COS,
  plus a management interface for the class space (`resctrl` on Linux)

\textsc{Streaming}, measured in `W6.1_IMPLEMENTATION_COST_AUDIT_2026-08-24.md`:
**no new architectural register, no new CPUID leaf, no context-switch action, no
class space to manage, no validity rule.** The label rides in a translation the
walker is already reading, in an encoding that is already there (PAT slot 6,
selected by PAT-selector/PCD/PWT). One bit through six structures, four lines of
decision logic, and on the OS side one line of new UAPI and 447 lines.

**The honest sentence is therefore about cost, not benefit:** the industry
shipped a larger mechanism, in the same place in the pipeline, for a class of
benefit no larger than ours — and it shipped it on a capability argument. We are
asking for strictly less silicon to make the same kind of decision about a
different kind of thing.

## Owed to the queue

- **Row 31**: do not write "CAT shipped for less benefit than this." Write the

## What this memo adds, and it is the part that matters

The 2026-08-23 memo refuses the claim and substitutes a measured comparison. It
does not source what CAT *is*. This memo does: the SDM Vol. 3B §17 language on
allocation, COS tagging, way-mask mapping and the contiguity rule, read at
source — which is what licenses the sharp form of the argument, that **CAT's
mechanism is H2's mechanism**: a per-request label consulted in the LLC
allocation decision, shipping since E5-2600 v3. That retires the WC/MOVNTDQA
analogy on evidence rather than preference, and it is the basis of queue row 30.

The verification-status table above is also new, and it is the reason this memo
should not simply be deleted in favour of the older one: three of its claims are
search-summary-only, one (**"CAT hits are unrestricted regardless of mask"**) is
explicitly marked not confirmed and not to be claimed, and one source is a
vendor blog. Without that table the SDM material would look uniformly solid.

## The measurements this memo lacked — fold these in

From `W5.3_L5_EVIDENCE_2026-08-23.md` via the 2026-08-23 memo. Every number is
ours and in-repo, and together they are a stronger argument than anything from
the literature:

| | measured |
|---|---|
| mos182 co-run residual under CAT12 | **1.00×** — CAT recovers the Intel tax completely |
| mos182 CAT price with **no co-runner present** | **1.222×** — intrinsic; a way mask shrinks the victim's own capacity |
| moscxl residual under CAT | **12.4×** — AMD's harm is rate-class; a capacity knob cannot reach it |
| `tab:fused`, streamer fused into the victim's own thread | **214.6 → 215.0 Mtuple/s** — CAT recovers nothing |

The last row is the one that bites, and it is why the argument does not depend
on the benefit magnitude being large. CAT is core- and class-scoped; when the
streaming reads issue from the victim's own thread there is no boundary for it
to draw. **Neither knob can name a region.**

The 2026-08-23 memo's summary sentence should be used, with one word fixed:

> A way-mask partitioner is a substantially larger hardware investment than a
> PAT encoding, it charges 22% of the victim's own performance to use, it is
> vendor-specific in what class of harm it can reach, and it cannot be aimed at
> an object at all.

Its original continuation — *"One PTE bit and two fill-path predicates can be"* —
must not be used. **There is no new PTE bit**; the decode reads the existing PAT
selector with PCD and PWT. Write *"A PAT encoding and two fill-path predicates
can be."* Same rhetorical shape, and it is the stronger claim.
