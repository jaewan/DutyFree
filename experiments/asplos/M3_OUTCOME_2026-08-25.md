# M3 outcome: non-allocation removes 27.4% of the stream's harm — partial, and the registered follow-up is a sweep

Pre-registration `M3_FLUSHBEHIND_PREREG_2026-08-25.md` (`36843be`), analyzer
committed before the data (`0c2c001`). mos181, victim `pointer_chase` 170 MB on
cpu8, F = 16 workers, hit rate 1.0, n=6, order rotated, 24/24 records.

| arm | n | victim cyc/load | CoV | harm |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| V alone | 6 | 78.080 | 0.06% | 1.0000 |
| **V+F_alloc** (read **and** retain) | 6 | 216.306 | 0.38% | **2.7703** |
| **V+F_fb** (read, flush behind 256 KiB) | 6 | 178.294 | 0.33% | **2.2835** |
| **V+F_ns** (not read at all) | 6 | 77.578 | 0.02% | **0.9936** |

    recovery = (2.7703 - 2.2835) / (2.7703 - 0.9936) = 0.4868 / 1.7767 = 27.4%

**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Neither pre-registered branch fires. Per the
pre-registration, the fraction alone may be claimed, and *"a mid-range result
under a saturating metric is a reason to sweep the flush distance before
interpreting, not to interpolate."*

Both anchors reproduced across campaigns, which is what makes the ratio
trustworthy: V at 78.080 against 78.08 / 78.079 / 78.18 in three prior runs, and
V+F_ns at 77.578 against M2's 77.552.

## What 27.4% means, stated carefully

Declining to *retain* the stream — while still reading every byte at full speed —
removes about a quarter of the harm it causes a neighbour. Roughly three quarters
of the harm survives non-allocation.

Two readings remain open, and they have opposite consequences:

1. **The proxy is leaky.** `clflushopt` evicts lines *after* they are allocated,
   and it is asynchronous. At 16 threads streaming ~8 GB/s the flush may simply
   not keep up, so the stream's true residency is far above the ~256 KiB per
   thread the distance nominally bounds. If so, a hardware H2 — which never
   allocates at all — would recover much more than 27.4%, and this figure is a
   loose lower bound.
2. **Most of the harm is bytes in flight.** Memory-controller queues, fabric
   occupancy, and DRAM/CXL bank contention do not care whether the line is kept.
   If so, 27.4% is close to the true residency share, no admission-control
   mechanism can reach the other ~73%, and H2 buys a quarter of the problem.

**The sweep separates them.** If recovery rises as the flush distance shrinks,
reading (1) is right and the proxy was under-flushing. If recovery is flat across
a 32× range of distances, reading (2) is right and 27.4% is the residency share.

## The price, recorded as registered

Flush-behind is per-core work the streamer pays and a hardware H2 would not.
Measured on F itself at 64 MiB fact: cost per access **58.32 → 68.22 cyc**
(+17.0%) and stream rate **2.024 → 1.733 GB/s** (−14.4%). The published AMD
cross-process arm paid 31.34% of its bandwidth for the same trick.

That gap is the standing argument for putting the policy in the memory type
rather than in software — and it holds regardless of how the sweep reads, because
it is a statement about the *proxy's* cost, not about the mechanism's benefit.

## Where this leaves the claim

Not "H2 works". Not "H2 is useless". The honest current statement:

> A fused tenant's stream degrades a latency-critical neighbour **2.77×**.
> Declining to retain that stream, while still reading it, removes **27.4%** of
> the degradation on this platform with a software proxy that costs the streamer
> 14–17%. Whether the remaining 73% is reachable by an admission-control
> mechanism at all is undetermined, and one sweep decides it.
